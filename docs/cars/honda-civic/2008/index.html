Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2008 Honda Civic reliable?

About average by government complaints. It drew about the average number of complaints filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 108; 100 is average, lower is better). The Honda Civic year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2011.

What are the most common 2008 Honda Civic problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are visibility (26%), suspension (20%) and engine (11%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2008 Honda Civic have?

7 safety recalls (latest: airbags). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2008 Honda Civic get?

24 to 42 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

At what mileage do 2008 Honda Civic problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 23,000 and 96,000 miles (37,000–154,000 km) (the middle half of the 735 complaints, of all 987 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K miles, a 2008 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 100K and 150K miles, a 2008 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2008 Honda Civic have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 8% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2008 Honda Civic cost in fuel a year?

About $1,550 to $3,300 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2008 Honda Civic have?

1.3L 4-cyl, 1.8L 4-cyl, 2.0L 4-cyl.

What Honda Civic year is better than the 2008?

Nearby, the 2011 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (about a quarter as many complaints as the average car).

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 19V502000 (airbags), 19V378000 (airbags) and 18V268000 (airbags) done? It has 7 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Do the wipers, washers, defrost and mirrors all work?
  3. Any clunks over bumps or uneven tire wear? Were struts, shocks or control arms replaced?
  4. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  5. Does the A/C blow cold and the heat work? Ask whether the A/C compressor or condenser was replaced. (37 owners named a a/c & heat repair bill; the middle one was $700.)
  6. Honda sent dealers 81 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Install crankshaft pulley correctly to prevent oil pump damage”?

How to read this chart

Newest year first. Red = worse than average, gray = about average, too new (2023+: a partial score, marked “partial”) or too few complaints to score. #1, #2… = that year's YouTube rank among the 52 Honda Civic years with enough videos (#1 = the year owners and reviewers talk about most positively). It is not a reliability rank.

How the YouTube ranks work

YouTube rank: we read 2,162 YouTube videos about the Honda Civic, 1973 to 2026, and ranked every year by how much they praise it vs. how much they talk about problems. YouTube's favorite: 2025. Old cars rank lower partly because most of their videos are repair and restoration videos. A gray dot means not enough data yet: under 10 videos, or a new year with no owner complaints to compare.

How to read these numbers

The Lemon Index counts owner complaints filed with the U.S. government (NHTSA) in a car's first 3 years, per car sold, against the average car of the same model years (100 = average, lower is better). It measures complaints, not crash safety. A complaint is an owner's report, not a confirmed defect. How it works

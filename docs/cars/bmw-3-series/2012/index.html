What a 2012 BMW 3 Series is worth

Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2012 BMW 3 Series reliable?

Yes, it is one of the most reliable by government complaints. It drew about a quarter as many complaints as the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 24; 100 is average, lower is better). The BMW 3 Series year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2022.

What are the most common 2012 BMW 3 Series problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are engine (42%), electrical (19%) and transmission & drivetrain (19%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2012 BMW 3 Series have?

8 safety recalls (latest: engine). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2012 BMW 3 Series get?

20 to 27 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2012 BMW 3 Series safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style.

At what mileage do 2012 BMW 3 Series problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 36,000 and 106,000 miles (58,000–171,000 km) (the middle half of the 106 complaints, of all 184 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K and 100K miles, a 2012 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 150K miles, a 2012 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2012 BMW 3 Series have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 19% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2012 BMW 3 Series cost in fuel a year?

About $2,950 to $4,000 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2012 BMW 3 Series have?

2.0L 4-cyl, 3.0L 6-cyl.

Is there a better BMW 3 Series year than the 2012?

Not nearby: the 2012 drew the fewest complaints per car sold of the BMW 3 Series years within 3 model years of it. Across every year, 2022 drew the fewest.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 26V733000 (engine), 24V608000 (engine) and 24V513000 (airbags) done? It has 8 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  3. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  4. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  5. Any fuel smell, stalling or hard starts? On a hybrid or EV, any reduced-power warning or battery work? (11 owners named a fuel system repair bill; the middle one was $4,000.)
  6. BMW sent dealers 599 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Engine Oil Quick Reference Chart and Drain Plug Torque: The current version of ISTA/D has an…”?

How to read this chart

Newest year first. Red = worse than average, gray = about average, too new (2023+: a partial score, marked “partial”) or too few complaints to score. #1, #2… = that year's YouTube rank among the 24 BMW 3 Series years with enough videos (#1 = the year owners and reviewers talk about most positively). It is not a reliability rank.

How the YouTube ranks work

YouTube rank: we read 778 YouTube videos about the BMW 3 Series, 1999 to 2025, and ranked every year by how much they praise it vs. how much they talk about problems. YouTube's favorite: 2021. Old cars rank lower partly because most of their videos are repair and restoration videos. A gray dot means not enough data yet: under 10 videos, or a new year with no owner complaints to compare.

How to read these numbers

The Lemon Index counts owner complaints filed with the U.S. government (NHTSA) in a car's first 3 years, per car sold, against the average car of the same model years (100 = average, lower is better). It measures complaints, not crash safety. A complaint is an owner's report, not a confirmed defect. How it works

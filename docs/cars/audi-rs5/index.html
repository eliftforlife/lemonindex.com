Known issues

  • Carbon buildup on intake valvesParts seller

    Direct injection means fuel never washes the intake valves, so carbon builds up. Signs are weak acceleration, misfires and higher fuel use.

    Fix: Pull the intake manifold and clean the valves; one shop lists $1,295 and up for an RS 5.

  • Injector failureOwner-reported

    An owner FAQ reports injectors that fail suddenly and can flood a cylinder; one car threw a cylinder 3 misfire at about 62,000 miles.

    Fix: Replace the injectors; that owner replaced all eight.

  • PCV valve diaphragmsOwner-reported

    The two PCV valves use rubber diaphragms that can tear, per an owner FAQ.

Known issues

  • Turbo oil feed line leaksParts seller

    A parts seller says the turbo oil feed line can leak, mainly when oil changes are stretched.

    Fix: The seller advises oil changes about every 5,000 miles.

  • Heat wear on hoses and intercooler partsParts seller

    The same seller lists heat-related wear on hoses and intercooler parts in the tightly packed engine.

  • PCV and crankcase pressureParts seller

    PCV and crankcase pressure faults can cause a rough idle, per the same seller.

  • Carbon buildupParts seller

    Direct injection and short trips lead to carbon on the intake side, noticeable past 60,000 miles on B9 cars, the seller says.

Quick answers

How fast is the Audi RS 5 Competition?

The B9 RS 5 Competition makes 444 hp and 442 lb-ft. The maker quotes 0–60 mph in 3.7 seconds. Top speed: 180 mph (limited).

What engine does the Audi RS 5 have?

The B9 RS 5 (2018–2025) uses the Audi 2.9 TFSI V6, a 2.9-liter V-6, twin-turbo. Older generations: B8: Audi 4.2 FSI V8.

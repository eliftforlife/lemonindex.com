When problems show up

Most problems on this year showed up between 13,000 and 64,000 miles (21,000–103,000 km) (the middle half of the 204 complaints, of all 379 filed so far, that listed a mileage).

  • At 60K miles (97K km) Right in the range where most owners reported problems.

This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not the odds that one car will have it.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 25V614000 (seat belts), 25V572000 (back over prevention) and 22V082000 (back over prevention) done? It has 5 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. NHTSA has an open investigation into desiccated air bag inflator rupture. Has this car had that problem?
  3. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  4. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  5. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  6. Does the A/C blow cold and the heat work? Ask whether the A/C compressor or condenser was replaced. (12 owners named a a/c & heat repair bill; the middle one was $2,200.)
  7. Ford sent dealers 167 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Some 2015 and 2016 mustang vehicles equipped with a 5.2l twin independent variable camshaft…”?

What to ask the shop

  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10091432: “Some 2016 f150, mustang and explorer vehicles equipped with a 3.5l gtdi, 2.3l gtdi or 2.7l gtdi engine built on or…”)
  • Screen, radio & camera: ask for the radio / screen software update first; it often fixes freezes and reboots without new parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10095648: “Some 2016 edge, f150, explorer, and mustang vehicles equipped with a myford display 4 inch screen may exhibit a blank…”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

Quick answers

Is the 2016 Ford Mustang reliable?

About average by government complaints. It drew about the average number of complaints filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 95; 100 is average, lower is better). 2016 is one of the better years of a model that draws 1.4× the average car's complaints. The Ford Mustang year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2010.

What are the most common 2016 Ford Mustang problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are engine (26%), transmission & drivetrain (16%) and electrical (15%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2016 Ford Mustang have?

5 safety recalls (latest: seat belts). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2016 Ford Mustang get?

18 to 25 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2016 Ford Mustang safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style, and the full table is on the advanced page.

At what mileage do 2016 Ford Mustang problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 13,000 and 64,000 miles (21,000–103,000 km) (the middle half of the 204 complaints, of all 379 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K miles, a 2016 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 100K and 150K miles, a 2016 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2016 Ford Mustang have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 16% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2016 Ford Mustang cost in fuel a year?

About $2,550 to $3,550 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2016 Ford Mustang have?

2.3L 4-cyl, 3.7L 6-cyl, 5.0L 8-cyl.

What Ford Mustang year is better than the 2016?

Nearby, the 2019 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (about two-thirds as many complaints as the average car). Across every year, 2010 drew the fewest.

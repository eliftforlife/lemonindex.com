What to ask the shop

  • Engine: ask whether an engine special coverage or warranty extension applies. Covered repairs can be free. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10209641: “Customer satisfaction notification P30 Engine Oil Filter Adapter”)
  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10054186: “Dodge: malfunction indicator lamp (mil) illuminates due to diagnostic trouble code (dtc) being set, p0606-internal…”)
  • Screen, radio & camera: ask for the radio / screen software update first; it often fixes freezes and reboots without new parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10058300: “Certain vehicles are having radio, navigational issues requiring software update”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2014 Ram 1500 reliable?

No, it is worse than average by government complaints. It drew about one and a half times the average car's complaints filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 139; 100 is average, lower is better). The Ram 1500 year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2018. This score is the whole line: 45% of its complaints that year are 2500/3500, gas and diesel mixed in. For a 2500/3500 or a diesel, see the Ram 2500/3500 page, by engine.

Is this score valid for a 2014 Ram 2500/3500 or a diesel?

No. Automakers report 2500/3500 sales together with the half-ton, so we can't give heavy-duty trucks their own per-truck score. The 2014 Ram 2500/3500 page shows their owner complaints split by engine (from each complaint's VIN), and each diesel has its own page.

What are the most common 2014 Ram 1500 problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are transmission & drivetrain (19%), engine (18%) and steering (15%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 23V411000 (engine), 22V406000 (fuel system, diesel) and 20V475000 (engine) done? It has 17 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. NHTSA has an open investigation into post recall remedy brake transmission shift interlock (btsi) failure. Has this car had that problem?
  3. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  4. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  5. Any steering noise, wander or power-steering warning? Has the steering rack or pump been replaced?
  6. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced? (136 owners named a electrical repair bill; the middle one was $1,300.)
  7. Ram sent dealers 615 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Axle assembly, axle carrier, axle ring and pinion engineering is currently investigating concerns”?

How the YouTube ranks work

YouTube rank: we read 790 YouTube videos about the Ram pickup, 1994 to 2025, and ranked every year by how much they praise it vs. how much they talk about problems. YouTube's favorite: 2020. Old cars rank lower partly because most of their videos are repair and restoration videos. A gray dot means not enough data yet: under 10 videos, or a new year with no owner complaints to compare.

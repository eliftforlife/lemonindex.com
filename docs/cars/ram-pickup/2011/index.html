When problems show up

Most problems on this year showed up between 30,000 and 102,000 miles (49,000–164,000 km) (the middle half of the 745 complaints, of all 1,212 filed so far, that listed a mileage).

  • At 60K and 100K miles (97K and 161K km) Right in the range where most owners reported problems.
  • At 150K miles (241K km) Past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most.

By area: steering mostly 24,000–71,000 miles (39,000–115,000 km); electrical mostly 60,000–110,000 miles (96,000–177,000 km).

This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not the odds that one car will have it.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Any steering noise, wander or power-steering warning? Has the steering rack or pump been replaced?
  2. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  3. Any clunks over bumps or uneven tire wear? Were struts, shocks or control arms replaced?
  4. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced? (136 owners named a electrical repair bill; the middle one was $1,300.)
  5. Ram sent dealers 250 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Advanced Field Action Communication ?”?

What to ask the shop

  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10044183: “Chrysler: this bulletin involves selectively erasing and reprogramming the engine control module (ecm) with new software”)
  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10042893: “Dodge: information regarding transmission control module (tcm) reprogramming with new software”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

Quick answers

Is the 2011 Ram 1500 reliable?

No, it is worse than average by government complaints. It drew about twice the average car's complaints filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 188; 100 is average, lower is better). The Ram 1500 year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2018. This score is the whole line: 65% of its complaints that year are 2500/3500, gas and diesel mixed in. For a 2500/3500 or a diesel, see the Ram 2500/3500 page, by engine.

Is this score valid for a 2011 Ram 2500/3500 or a diesel?

No. Automakers report 2500/3500 sales together with the half-ton, so we can't give heavy-duty trucks their own per-truck score. The 2011 Ram 2500/3500 page shows their owner complaints split by engine (from each complaint's VIN), and each diesel has its own page.

What are the most common 2011 Ram 1500 problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are steering (59%), transmission & drivetrain (21%) and suspension (11%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2011 Ram 1500 have?

None: NHTSA has no safety recalls on record for it.

What gas mileage does the 2011 Ram 1500 get?

15 to 16 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

At what mileage do 2011 Ram 1500 problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 30,000 and 102,000 miles (49,000–164,000 km) (the middle half of the 745 complaints, of all 1,212 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K and 100K miles, a 2011 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 150K miles, a 2011 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2011 Ram 1500 have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 21% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2011 Ram 1500 cost in fuel a year?

About $4,000 to $4,900 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2011 Ram 1500 have?

3.7L 6-cyl, 4.7L 8-cyl, 5.7L 8-cyl.

What Ram 1500 year is better than the 2011?

Nearby, the 2013 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (somewhat fewer complaints than the average car). Across every year, 2018 drew the fewest.

How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Quick answers

Is the 2008 Lincoln Navigator reliable?

We can't score it: only 14,836 were sold in the U.S. in 2008 (calendar-year sales), under the 20,000 we need for a fair per-car score. Owners filed 9 complaints with NHTSA in its first 3 years.

What are the most common 2008 Lincoln Navigator problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are transmission & drivetrain (22%), visibility (22%) and equipment & accessories (11%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2008 Lincoln Navigator have?

2 safety recalls (latest: tires). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2008 Lincoln Navigator get?

14 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Does the 2008 Lincoln Navigator have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 22% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2008 Lincoln Navigator cost in fuel a year?

About $4,300 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2008 Lincoln Navigator have?

5.4L 8-cyl.

What Lincoln Navigator year is better than the 2008?

No year within 3 model years of it drew fewer complaints. Across every year, 2004 drew the fewest (somewhat fewer complaints than the average car).

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 08V166000 (tires) and 08V058000 (latches/locks/linkages) done? Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  3. Do the wipers, washers, defrost and mirrors all work?
  4. Lincoln sent dealers 28 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Oil separation from the internal lube found in the unitized cartridge seal”?

How the YouTube ranks work

Old cars rank lower partly because most of their videos are repair and restoration videos. A gray dot means not enough data yet: under 10 videos, or a new year with no owner complaints to compare.

Known issues

  • Turbo failureParts seller

    A parts seller says the twin turbos wear out with age even on stock cars, and fail sooner on tuned ones. They sit deep in the engine bay.

    Fix: Ask whether the turbos were ever replaced. Owners often do them with the engine out.

    3 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (2000–2002)
  • Timing belt serviceParts seller

    The 2.7T uses a timing belt, not a chain, and it is hard to reach without taking off the bumper and front support.

    Fix: Without proof of a recent belt job, plan one right away, with new tensioners.

  • Valve cover gasket oil leaksParts seller

    Gaskets on the tightly packed engine age and leak, and the source of a leak can be hard to find.

    Fix: Replace the valve cover gaskets with a full kit, ideally while the engine is out.

    5 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (2000–2002)

Known issues

  • Timing chain guides and tensionersOwner-reported

    The cam chains sit at the back of the engine. Owners report worn or broken plastic guides and tensioners, often with a rattle at cold start. A parts seller says failures can start at 50,000–80,000 miles.

    Fix: Owners report the engine has to come out for the job, with bills around $5,000–$7,000.

    8 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (2004–2005)
  • Mechanical cam adjusterParts seller

    The same seller says a worn mechanical cam adjuster can make one bank of cylinders misfire and can damage the electric adjuster.

    Fix: It is replaced as part of a timing chain service kit.

    1 owner complaint to NHTSA mentions it (2004–2005)

Known issues

  • Fuel rail leak recall (2011–2012)Recall

    On some 2011–2012 S4s, the fuel injection system could leak fuel, which raises the risk of fire.

    Fix: Dealer replaces the fuel rails and seals for free (NHTSA 15V-019).

    2 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (2010–2016)
  • Water pump leaksParts seller / class action (alleged)

    A parts seller says the pump sometimes leaks from its weep hole. A 2022 class action named 2013–2022 S4s with the 3.0T and alleges early bearing and seal failure.

    Fix: Replace the pump. The seller also advises a new pump after any overheating.

    11 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (2010–2016)
  • Oil use on early enginesParts seller

    Early engines built before mid-2012 could use extra oil, per a parts seller.

    Fix: An updated PCV valve, and in rare cases new piston rings.

    1 owner complaint to NHTSA mentions it (2010–2016)
  • Carbon buildupParts seller

    With direct injection, fuel never washes the intake valves, so carbon collects. Signs are weak acceleration, misfires and higher fuel use.

    Fix: Clean the valves with the intake off; one shop lists $1,195 and up for a B8 S4.

    1 owner complaint to NHTSA mentions it (2010–2016)

Known issues

  • Water pump and coolant module leaksClass action (alleged)

    Two class actions say the 3.0T water pump wears out early. The 2022 suit names 2013–2022 S4s. A 2026 suit over EA839 engines says coolant can leak inside the module, out of sight.

    Fix: A parts seller says the plastic impeller can come off the shaft, and lists the pump at $300–$500 plus labor.

    1 owner complaint to NHTSA mentions it (2018–2025)
  • Auxiliary water pumpParts seller

    The electric pump that cools the turbo after shutdown can fail, often around 40,000–60,000 miles, per the seller.

    Fix: Replace it along with the main pump, since the labor overlaps.

  • PCV diaphragm in the valve coverParts seller

    A torn PCV diaphragm can cause oil use and blue smoke, per the same seller.

    Fix: Replace the valve cover, which holds the PCV; listed at $800–$1,300.

    1 owner complaint to NHTSA mentions it (2018–2025)
  • Carbon buildupParts seller

    The S4's 3.0T has no port injection, so carbon builds on the intake valves. The seller says it shows after 60,000–80,000 miles.

    Fix: Walnut blasting; the seller lists $400–$800 at a shop.

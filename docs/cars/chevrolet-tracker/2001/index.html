How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2001 Chevrolet Tracker reliable?

Yes, better than average by government complaints. It drew about two-thirds as many complaints as the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 67; 100 is average, lower is better). The Chevrolet Tracker year with the fewest complaints per car sold is 2002.

What are the most common 2001 Chevrolet Tracker problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are engine (22%), brakes (19%) and steering (16%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2001 Chevrolet Tracker have?

None: NHTSA has no safety recalls on record for it.

What gas mileage does the 2001 Chevrolet Tracker get?

17 to 22 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Does the 2001 Chevrolet Tracker have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 8% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2001 Chevrolet Tracker cost in fuel a year?

About $2,750 to $3,550 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2001 Chevrolet Tracker have?

2.0L 4-cyl, 2.5L 6-cyl.

What Chevrolet Tracker year is better than the 2001?

Nearby, the 2002 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (about half as many complaints as the average car).

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  2. Do the brakes feel even, with no ABS or brake warning light? When were the pads and brake fluid last done?
  3. Any steering noise, wander or power-steering warning? Has the steering rack or pump been replaced?
  4. Chevy sent dealers 306 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Experiencing a tick type noise coming from the engine while the engine is running during cold…”?

Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2011 Nissan Sentra reliable?

Yes, it is one of the most reliable by government complaints. It drew about a third as many complaints as the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 35; 100 is average, lower is better).

What are the most common 2011 Nissan Sentra problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are engine (23%), electrical (17%) and airbags (9%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2011 Nissan Sentra have?

2 safety recalls (latest: electrical). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2011 Nissan Sentra get?

24 to 29 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2011 Nissan Sentra safe?

NHTSA gave it 4 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style.

At what mileage do 2011 Nissan Sentra problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 18,000 and 109,000 miles (29,000–176,000 km) (the middle half of the 116 complaints, of all 142 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K and 100K miles, a 2011 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 150K miles, a 2011 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2011 Nissan Sentra have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 9% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2011 Nissan Sentra cost in fuel a year?

About $2,200 to $3,300 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2011 Nissan Sentra have?

2.0L 4-cyl, 2.5L 4-cyl.

Is there a better Nissan Sentra year than the 2011?

Not nearby: the 2011 drew the fewest complaints per car sold of the Nissan Sentra years within 3 model years of it.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 11V579000 (electrical) and 10V555000 (electrical) done? Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  3. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  4. Is the airbag light off, and were any airbag recalls done?
  5. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records. (11 owners named a transmission repair bill; the middle one was $3,500.)
  6. Nissan sent dealers 107 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Oil is leaking from the upper end of the engine oil cooler”?

How to read these numbers

The Lemon Index counts owner complaints filed with the U.S. government (NHTSA) in a car's first 3 years, per car sold, against the average car of the same model years (100 = average, lower is better). It measures complaints, not crash safety. A complaint is an owner's report, not a confirmed defect. How it works

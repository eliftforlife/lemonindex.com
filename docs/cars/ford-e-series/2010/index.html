How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2010 Ford E-Series reliable?

Yes, it is one of the most reliable by government complaints. It drew less than a sixth as many complaints as the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 7; 100 is average, lower is better).

What are the most common 2010 Ford E-Series problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are electrical (38%), speed control (25%) and suspension (25%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2010 Ford E-Series have?

18 safety recalls (latest: equipment adaptive/mobility). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

Does the 2010 Ford E-Series have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues aren't among its top 5 complaint areas.

Is there a better Ford E-Series year than the 2010?

Not nearby: the 2010 drew the fewest complaints per car sold of the Ford E-Series years within 3 model years of it.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 16V350000 (equipment adaptive/mobility), 15V779000 (equipment adaptive/mobility) and 15V763000 (equipment adaptive/mobility) done? It has 18 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  3. Does it accelerate and hold speed smoothly, with no surging or stalling?
  4. Any clunks over bumps or uneven tire wear? Were struts, shocks or control arms replaced?
  5. Ford sent dealers 35 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Steering wander”?

How to read these numbers

The Lemon Index counts owner complaints filed with the U.S. government (NHTSA) in a car's first 3 years, per car sold, against the average car of the same model years (100 = average, lower is better).

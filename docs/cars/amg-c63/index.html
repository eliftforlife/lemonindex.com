Known issues

  • Cylinder head boltsMaker bulletin

    Head bolts can stretch and snap on engines up to serial 060658 (built to about mid-2010), letting coolant into the cylinders. Mercedes covered it in an April 2011 bulletin.

    Fix: Replace all head bolts and the hydraulic lifters; many owners fit studs.

  • Camshaft and lifter wearMaker bulletin

    Intake cam lobes can wear into the lifters, often near 100,000 miles, with clatter at cold start. Mercedes acknowledged the wear in a 2009 bulletin; a U.S. class action over it did not proceed.

    Fix: Replace worn camshafts and lifters, and change the oil often.

  • Intake manifoldParts seller

    The center plate of the magnesium intake manifold, which holds the throttle bodies, deteriorates and causes rough running at idle.

    Fix: Replace the manifold or fit a center-plate repair kit.

  • Crankcase breather valveParts seller

    The breather valve diaphragm or its hose cracks, raising oil burning and setting a P0170 fuel trim code.

    Fix: Replace the breather valve; the intake manifold has to come off.

Known issues

  • Cam adjuster wearParts seller

    Old or poor oil can leave varnish that slows the cam adjusters, causing a rough cold idle, startup rattle and P0011/P0021 codes.

    Fix: Change the oil on time with an approved 5W-40 full synthetic.

  • Spark plugs and coilsParts seller

    Boost wears spark plugs fast. Misfires and hesitation under boost are the signs.

    Fix: Replace plugs on schedule, and coils as a set if one fails.

    1 owner complaint to NHTSA mention it (2015–2022)
  • Turbo oil line seepageParts seller

    Heat cycling can make the turbo oil feed line fittings seep, often after 50,000 miles.

    Fix: Check the lines at each service and replace any that seep.

  • Water pump and thermostatParts seller

    The water pump and electric thermostat can fail, usually after 70,000 miles, causing coolant loss and overheating.

    Fix: Replace the pump and thermostat together.

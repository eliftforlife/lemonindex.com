Known issues

  • Valve stem seals and oil useOutlet + parts seller

    Heat from the turbos in the V can harden the valve stem seals. Owners report blue smoke and oil use between services, mostly at high miles.

    Fix: Replace the valve stem seals.

  • Turbo failuresOutlet + parts seller

    Turbos can fail when the oil is old or dirty. The parts seller says first-gen S63 turbos fail more often than later ones.

    Fix: Short oil change intervals help.

  • Rod bearing wearOutlet + parts seller

    Rod bearing wear is a known complaint on early S63 engines, especially when driven hard. The parts seller suggests changing them early on E70/E71 cars.

    Fix: Preventive rod bearing replacement.

    1 owner complaint to NHTSA mentions it (2010–2013 (X6 M to 2014))
  • Auxiliary water pump recall (2010–2012)Recall

    The circuit board in the turbo coolant pump could short and overheat. In rare cases this could start a fire. It covers X5 M (E70) and X6 M (E71) cars built through November 2011.

    Fix: Dealer replaces the pump for free (NHTSA 18V-248).

    2 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (2010–2013 (X6 M to 2014))

Quick answers

How fast is the BMW X5 M Competition?

The F95 / F96 LCI X5 M Competition makes 617 hp and 553 lb-ft. MotorTrend measured 0–60 mph in 3.4 seconds. Top speed: 155 mph (limited; 177 with M Driver’s Package).

What engine does the BMW X5 M / X6 M have?

The F95 / F96 LCI X5 M (2024–) uses the BMW S68, a 4.4-liter V8, twin-turbo, 48-volt mild hybrid. Older generations: E70 / E71: BMW S63; F85 / F86: BMW S63; F95 / F96: BMW S63.

Which BMW X5 M / X6 M engine do YouTube owners like most?

BMW S68 55/100, BMW S63 54/100. That's the tone of the most-watched videos about each engine, praise words against trouble words.

What to ask the shop

  • Engine: ask whether an engine special coverage or warranty extension applies. Covered repairs can be free. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10090095: “Dealer parts and service manager memo: wty007 - 2011-2014 my sportage 2.0l turbo gdi & 2.4l gdi engine - effective…”)
  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10089786: “Voluntary service campaign: ecm upgrade - mil on with dtc p0087 (sc114) - this bulletin provides information…”)
  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10090431: “6-speed a/t transmission adaptive values reset/relearn - this bulletin provides the procedures necessary to reset…”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Recalls & safety

Recalls 3

  • Brakes · recall issued 2023

    The Hydraulic Electronic Control Unit (HECU) may experience an electrical short, which can result in an engine compartment fire while parked or driving.

  • Fuel system, gasoline · recall issued 2018

    The high pressure fuel pipe that connects to the fuel pump outlet may have been damaged, misaligned or improperly torqued during the engine replacement procedure, possibly causing a fuel leak.

  • Engine · recall issued 2017

    Machining errors during the engine manufacturing process may cause premature bearing wear within the engine.

Recall repairs are free at any dealer. A recall only matters if it's still open on that exact car, so check its VIN.

Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2013 Kia Sportage reliable?

Yes, it is one of the most reliable by government complaints. It drew about half as many complaints as the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 49; 100 is average, lower is better). The Kia Sportage year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2010.

What are the most common 2013 Kia Sportage problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are engine (33%), brakes (24%) and electrical (19%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2013 Kia Sportage have?

3 safety recalls (latest: brakes). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2013 Kia Sportage get?

21 to 25 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2013 Kia Sportage safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style, and the full table is on the advanced page.

At what mileage do 2013 Kia Sportage problems show up?

How much does the 2013 Kia Sportage cost in fuel a year?

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 23V652000 (brakes), 18V907000 (fuel system, gasoline) and 17V224000 (engine) done? Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. NHTSA has an open investigation into hyundai and kia abs module fires. Has this car had that problem?
  3. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  4. Do the brakes feel even, with no ABS or brake warning light? When were the pads and brake fluid last done?
  5. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  6. Kia sent dealers 167 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Smart pedal feature - the smart pedal is an integral part of the engine management system (ems)”?

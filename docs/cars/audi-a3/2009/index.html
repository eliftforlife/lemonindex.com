Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2009 Audi A3 reliable?

We can't score it: only 3,874 were sold in the U.S. in 2009 (calendar-year sales), under the 20,000 we need for a fair per-car score. Owners filed 3 complaints with NHTSA in its first 3 years.

What are the most common 2009 Audi A3 problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are transmission & drivetrain (67%), electrical (33%) and engine (33%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2009 Audi A3 have?

5 safety recalls (latest: airbags). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2009 Audi A3 get?

21 to 24 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Does the 2009 Audi A3 have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 67% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2009 Audi A3 cost in fuel a year?

About $3,300 to $3,800 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2009 Audi A3 have?

2.0L 4-cyl, 3.2L 6-cyl.

What Audi A3 year is better than the 2009?

No year within 3 model years of it drew fewer complaints. Across every year, 2016 drew the fewest (about half as many complaints as the average car).

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 18V082000 (airbags), 16V913000 (brakes) and 16V079000 (airbags) done? It has 5 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  3. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  4. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.

How to read these numbers

The Lemon Index counts owner complaints filed with the U.S. government (NHTSA) in a car's first 3 years, per car sold, against the average car of the same model years (100 = average, lower is better). It measures complaints, not crash safety. A complaint is an owner's report, not a confirmed defect. How it works

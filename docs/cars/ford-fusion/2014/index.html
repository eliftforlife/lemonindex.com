Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 26V376000 (transmission & drivetrain), 23V162000 (brakes) and 22V413000 (transmission & drivetrain) done? It has 19 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. NHTSA has an open investigation into desiccated air bag inflator rupture. Has this car had that problem?
  3. Any steering noise, wander or power-steering warning? Has the steering rack or pump been replaced?
  4. Do all the seat belts latch and retract?
  5. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  6. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records. (153 owners named a engine repair bill; the middle one was $7,900.)
  7. Ford sent dealers 189 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Due to incorrect level of restraint control module (rcm) installed, was caused by an incorrect”?

What to ask the shop

  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10055640: “Lincoln/ford: on some vehicles, there are no upshift, only reverse and first gear available, which is due to a software…”)
  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10153973: “Some 2013-2014 Fusion and MKZ vehicles may not complete a PMI procedure when installing a FCIM”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

Quick answers

Is the 2014 Ford Fusion reliable?

About average by government complaints. It drew about the average number of complaints filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 101; 100 is average, lower is better). 2014 is one of the better years of a model that draws 1.9× the average car's complaints. The Ford Fusion year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2018.

What are the most common 2014 Ford Fusion problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are steering (27%), seat belts (14%) and electrical (13%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2014 Ford Fusion have?

19 safety recalls (latest: transmission & drivetrain). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2014 Ford Fusion get?

25 to 41 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2014 Ford Fusion safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style, and the full table is on the advanced page.

At what mileage do 2014 Ford Fusion problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 41,000 and 102,000 miles (66,000–164,000 km) (the middle half of the 917 complaints, of all 1,430 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K and 100K miles, a 2014 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 150K miles, a 2014 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2014 Ford Fusion have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 9% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2014 Ford Fusion cost in fuel a year?

About $1,550 to $2,550 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2014 Ford Fusion have?

1.5L 4-cyl, 2.0L 4-cyl, 2.5L 4-cyl.

What Ford Fusion year is better than the 2014?

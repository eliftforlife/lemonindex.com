Questions to ask the seller

  1. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  2. Has it ever braked hard for no reason? Ask whether the safety-system software was updated.
  3. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  4. Nissan sent dealers 30 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “VEHICLE KEY NOT DETECTED / AUTHENTICATED, ENGINE WILL NOT START This bulletin has been amended”?

Quick answers

Is the 2023 Nissan Altima reliable?

It's too early to be sure. So far it has drawn about a fifth as many complaints as the average car per car sold, against other 2023 cars after the same time on the road (early Lemon Index 19; 100 is average). The Nissan Altima year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2022.

What are the most common 2023 Nissan Altima problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are electrical (37%), automatic emergency braking (20%) and engine (20%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2023 Nissan Altima have?

None: NHTSA has no safety recalls on record for it.

What gas mileage does the 2023 Nissan Altima get?

29 to 31 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2023 Nissan Altima safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style, and the full table is on the advanced page.

Does the 2023 Nissan Altima have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues aren't among its top 5 complaint areas.

How much does the 2023 Nissan Altima cost in fuel a year?

About $2,050 to $2,200 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2023 Nissan Altima have?

2.0L 4-cyl, 2.5L 4-cyl.

What Nissan Altima year is better than the 2023?

Nearby, the 2022 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (about a fifth as many complaints as the average car).

The Lemon Index counts owner complaints filed with the U.S. government (NHTSA) in a car's first 3 years, per car sold, against the average car of the same model years (100 = average, lower is better).

What to ask the shop

  • Transmission: ask whether a transmission special coverage or warranty extension applies to this car. Covered repairs can be free. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10229597: “CVT Class Action Owner Notification In mid-December, current and former owners and lessees of the following vehicles…”)
  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10109163: “ECM Reprogram Voluntary Emissions Service Campaign This bulletin applies only to vehciles equipped with 4 cylinder engine”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2017 Nissan Altima reliable?

Yes, better than average by government complaints. It drew about half as many complaints as the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 52; 100 is average, lower is better). The Nissan Altima year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2022.

What are the most common 2017 Nissan Altima problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are transmission & drivetrain (37%), engine (11%) and speed control (9%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2017 Nissan Altima have?

3 safety recalls (latest: latches/locks/linkages). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2017 Nissan Altima get?

26 to 31 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2017 Nissan Altima safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style.

At what mileage do 2017 Nissan Altima problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 28,000 and 77,000 miles (45,000–124,000 km) (the middle half of the 165 complaints, of all 392 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K miles, a 2017 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 100K and 150K miles, a 2017 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2017 Nissan Altima have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 37% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2017 Nissan Altima cost in fuel a year?

About $2,050 to $2,450 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2017 Nissan Altima have?

2.5L 4-cyl, 3.5L 6-cyl.

Is there a better Nissan Altima year than the 2017?

Not nearby: the 2017 drew the fewest complaints per car sold of the Nissan Altima years within 3 model years of it. Across every year, 2022 drew the fewest.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 20V315000 (latches/locks/linkages), 18V915000 (body structure) and 17V040000 (latches/locks/linkages) done? Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. NHTSA has an open investigation into desiccated air bag inflator rupture. Has this car had that problem?
  3. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  4. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  5. Does it accelerate and hold speed smoothly, with no surging or stalling?
  6. Is the airbag light off, and were any airbag recalls done? (37 owners named a airbags repair bill; the middle one was $1,500.)
  7. Nissan sent dealers 247 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “NISSAN; OIL LEAKING AROUND VALVE TIMING CONTROL SOLENOID VALVE This bulletin applies to…”?

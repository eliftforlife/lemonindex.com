When problems show up

Most problems on this year showed up between 30,000 and 87,000 miles (48,000–140,000 km) (the middle half of the 358 complaints, of all 668 filed so far, that listed a mileage).

  • At 60K miles (97K km) Right in the range where most owners reported problems.
  • At 100K and 150K miles (161K and 241K km) Past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most.

By area: electrical mostly 26,000–68,000 miles (41,000–110,000 km); engine mostly 26,000–88,000 miles (42,000–142,000 km).

This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not the odds that one car will have it.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 14E007000 (engine), 07V063000 (lights) and 06V183000 (lights) done? It has 7 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  3. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  4. Is the airbag light off, and were any airbag recalls done?
  5. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records. (55 owners named a transmission repair bill; the middle one was $2,200.)
  6. Volkswagen sent dealers 240 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Subject regarding coolant leaking from coolant reservoir bottle wiring connector contaminates…”?

What to ask the shop

  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10021950: “Emissions recall circular”)
  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10156129: “Transmission Control Module Updates: This document informs of the software, and technical bulletins that service…”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

Quick answers

Is the 2001 Volkswagen Jetta reliable?

No, it is one of the worst by government complaints. It drew about 3 times the average car's complaints filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 251; 100 is average, lower is better). The Volkswagen Jetta year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2018.

What are the most common 2001 Volkswagen Jetta problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are engine (17%), electrical (16%) and airbags (16%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2001 Volkswagen Jetta have?

7 safety recalls (latest: engine). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2001 Volkswagen Jetta get?

19 to 33 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

At what mileage do 2001 Volkswagen Jetta problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 30,000 and 87,000 miles (48,000–140,000 km) (the middle half of the 358 complaints, of all 668 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K miles, a 2001 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 100K and 150K miles, a 2001 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2001 Volkswagen Jetta have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues aren't among its top 5 complaint areas.

How much does the 2001 Volkswagen Jetta cost in fuel a year?

About $2,700 to $3,600 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2001 Volkswagen Jetta have?

1.8L 4-cyl, 1.9L 4-cyl, 2.0L 4-cyl, 2.8L 6-cyl.

What Volkswagen Jetta year is better than the 2001?

No year within 3 model years of it drew fewer complaints. Across every year, 2018 drew the fewest (less than a sixth as many complaints as the average car).

What to ask the shop

  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10053737: “Kia: information regarding vehicles experiencing malfunction indicator lamp (mil) illuminating with electronic control…”)
  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10090431: “6-speed a/t transmission adaptive values reset/relearn - this bulletin provides the procedures necessary to reset…”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Quick answers

Is the 2011 Kia Sorento reliable?

No, it is worse than average by government complaints. It drew a bit more than the average car's complaints filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 121; 100 is average, lower is better). The Kia Sorento year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2018.

What are the most common 2011 Kia Sorento problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are transmission & drivetrain (40%), engine (16%) and electrical (16%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2011 Kia Sorento have?

8 safety recalls (latest: engine). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2011 Kia Sorento get?

20 to 24 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2011 Kia Sorento safe?

NHTSA gave it 4 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style, and the full table is on the advanced page.

At what mileage do 2011 Kia Sorento problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 38,000 and 115,000 miles (61,000–185,000 km) (the middle half of the 1,115 complaints, of all 1,520 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K and 100K miles, a 2011 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 150K miles, a 2011 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2011 Kia Sorento have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 40% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2011 Kia Sorento cost in fuel a year?

About $2,700 to $3,200 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2011 Kia Sorento have?

2.4L 4-cyl, 3.5L 6-cyl.

What Kia Sorento year is better than the 2011?

Nearby, the 2012 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (somewhat fewer complaints than the average car). Across every year, 2018 drew the fewest.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 23V877000 (engine), 23V652000 (brakes) and 15V626000 (transmission & drivetrain) done? It has 8 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. NHTSA has an open investigation into hyundai and kia abs module fires. Has this car had that problem?
  3. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  4. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  5. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  6. Is the airbag light off, and were any airbag recalls done? (49 owners named a airbags repair bill; the middle one was $2,000.)
  7. Kia sent dealers 202 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Service action-starter solenoid wire contact with coolant line bracket”?

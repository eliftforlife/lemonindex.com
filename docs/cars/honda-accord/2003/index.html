What to ask the shop

  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10014201: “Updating replacement ecm/pcm software”)
  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10039537: “Honda: the vehicle moves faster than expected with the transmission in gear, foot off the brake, and foot off the throttle”)

How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2003 Honda Accord reliable?

No, it is worse than average by government complaints. It drew about one and a half times the average car's complaints filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 150; 100 is average, lower is better). 2003 is one of the weaker years of a model that usually draws somewhat fewer complaints than the average car. The Honda Accord year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2011.

What are the most common 2003 Honda Accord problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are brakes (26%), transmission & drivetrain (14%) and airbags (11%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2003 Honda Accord have?

24 safety recalls (latest: speed control). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2003 Honda Accord get?

21 to 26 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

At what mileage do 2003 Honda Accord problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 51,000 and 119,000 miles (82,000–192,000 km) (the middle half of the 1,583 complaints, of all 1,987 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K and 100K miles, a 2003 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 150K miles, a 2003 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2003 Honda Accord have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 14% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2003 Honda Accord cost in fuel a year?

About $2,450 to $3,050 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2003 Honda Accord have?

2.4L 4-cyl, 3.0L 6-cyl.

What Honda Accord year is better than the 2003?

Nearby, the 2006 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (a bit under half as many complaints as the average car). Across every year, 2011 drew the fewest.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 19E068000 (speed control), 19V499000 (airbags) and 19V501000 (airbags) done? It has 24 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. NHTSA has an open investigation into desiccated air bag inflator rupture. Has this car had that problem?
  3. Do the brakes feel even, with no ABS or brake warning light? When were the pads and brake fluid last done?
  4. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  5. Is the airbag light off, and were any airbag recalls done?
  6. Honda sent dealers 382 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Automatic transmission leaks on the cooler lines”?

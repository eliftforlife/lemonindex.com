What to ask the shop

  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10140021: “Flash: Powertrain Diagnostic And System Enhancements This bulletin involves reprogramming the Powertrain Control Module…”)
  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10138925: “Flash: Transmission Diagnostic and Shift Enhancements This bulletin involves reprogramming the Transmission Control…”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Quick answers

Is the 2017 Dodge Charger reliable?

Yes, better than average by government complaints. It drew a bit over half as many complaints as the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Complaint Score 57; 100 is average, lower is better). The Dodge Charger year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2022.

What are the most common 2017 Dodge Charger problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are electrical (30%), body structure (20%) and fuel system (9%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2017 Dodge Charger have?

4 safety recalls (latest: electrical). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2017 Dodge Charger get?

16 to 23 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2017 Dodge Charger safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style.

At what mileage do 2017 Dodge Charger problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 19,000 and 65,000 miles (30,000–105,000 km) (the middle half of the 61 complaints, of all 128 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K miles, a 2017 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 100K and 150K miles, a 2017 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2017 Dodge Charger have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues aren't among its top 5 complaint areas.

How much does the 2017 Dodge Charger cost in fuel a year?

About $2,800 to $5,000 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2017 Dodge Charger have?

3.6L 6-cyl, 5.7L 8-cyl, 6.2L 8-cyl, 6.4L 8-cyl.

What Dodge Charger year is better than the 2017?

Nearby, the 2020 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (about a quarter as many complaints as the average car). Across every year, 2022 drew the fewest.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 18V332000 (electrical), 18V281000 (transmission & drivetrain) and 17V496000 (engine) done? It has 4 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. NHTSA has an open investigation into desiccated air bag inflator rupture. Has this car had that problem?
  3. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  4. Any rust or damage underneath, or doors and hatches that don't close right?
  5. Any fuel smell, stalling or hard starts? On a hybrid or EV, any reduced-power warning or battery work?
  6. Any steering noise, wander or power-steering warning? Has the steering rack or pump been replaced? (7 owners named a steering repair bill; the middle one was $2,000.)
  7. Dodge sent dealers 222 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Electric Power Steering Gear For LA, LD and LX Models ONLY; Before this part is replaced for:…”?

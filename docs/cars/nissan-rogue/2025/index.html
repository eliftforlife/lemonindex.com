Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2025 Nissan Rogue reliable?

It's too early to be sure. So far it has drawn about one and a half times the average car's complaints per car sold, against other 2025 cars after the same time on the road (early Lemon Index 140; 100 is average). 2025 is one of the weaker years of a model that usually draws a bit over half as many complaints as the average car. The Nissan Rogue year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2012.

What are the most common 2025 Nissan Rogue problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are wipers & visibility (25%), visibility (18%) and automatic emergency braking (7%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2025 Nissan Rogue have?

3 safety recalls (latest: fuel system, gasoline). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2025 Nissan Rogue get?

29 to 33 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2025 Nissan Rogue safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style.

At what mileage do 2025 Nissan Rogue problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 8,500 and 18,000 miles (14,000–29,000 km) (the middle half of the 23 complaints, of all 194 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K, 100K and 150K miles, a 2025 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2025 Nissan Rogue have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues aren't among its top 5 complaint areas.

How much does the 2025 Nissan Rogue cost in fuel a year?

About $1,950 to $2,200 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2025 Nissan Rogue have?

1.5L 3-cyl.

What Nissan Rogue year is better than the 2025?

Nearby, the 2022 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (about two-thirds as many complaints as the average car). Across every year, 2012 drew the fewest.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 26V080000 (engine), 26V081000 (fuel system, gasoline) and 24V748000 (back over prevention) done? Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Do the wipers, washers, defrost and mirrors all work?
  3. Has it ever braked hard for no reason? Ask whether the safety-system software was updated.
  4. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records. (18 owners named a engine repair bill; the middle one was $1,700.)
  5. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced? (14 owners named a electrical repair bill; the middle one was $2,600.)
  6. Nissan sent dealers 16 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Steering wheel switch buttons inoperative, sticking, or not clicking when pressed if you…”?

How the YouTube ranks work

YouTube rank: we read 597 YouTube videos about the Nissan Rogue, 2008 to 2025, and ranked every year by how much they praise it vs. how much they talk about problems. YouTube's favorite: 2025. Old cars rank lower partly because most of their videos are repair and restoration videos. A gray dot means not enough data yet: under 10 videos, or a new year with no owner complaints to compare.

How to read these numbers

The Lemon Index counts owner complaints filed with the U.S. government (NHTSA) in a car's first 3 years, per car sold, against the average car of the same model years (100 = average, lower is better). It measures complaints, not crash safety. A complaint is an owner's report, not a confirmed defect. How it works

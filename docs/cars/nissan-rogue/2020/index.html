When problems show up

Most problems on this year showed up between 1,100 and 26,000 miles (1,800–43,000 km) (the middle half of the 52 complaints, of all 152 filed so far, that listed a mileage).

  • At 60K, 100K and 150K miles (97K, 161K and 241K km) Past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most.

This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not the odds that one car will have it.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recall 23V093000 (electrical) done? Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Has it ever braked hard for no reason? Ask whether the safety-system software was updated.
  3. Do the brakes feel even, with no ABS or brake warning light? When were the pads and brake fluid last done?
  4. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  5. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records. (42 owners named a transmission repair bill; the middle one was $5,000.)
  6. Is the airbag light off, and were any airbag recalls done? (17 owners named a airbags repair bill; the middle one was $2,000.)
  7. Nissan sent dealers 122 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “POWER BACK DOOR WILL NOT OPERATE WITH REQUEST SWITCHES IF YOU CONFIRM On vehicles equipped…”?

What to ask the shop

  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10237563: “CVT/TCM CALIBRATION DATA WRITE PROCEDURE This bulletin has been amended”)
  • Screen, radio & camera: ask for the radio / screen software update first; it often fixes freezes and reboots without new parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10234049: “SOFTWARE UPDATE FOR INFOTAINMENT ISSUES This bulletin has been superseded by: NTB23-018 SOFTWARE UPDATE FOR…”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

Quick answers

Is the 2020 Nissan Rogue reliable?

Yes, it is one of the most reliable by government complaints. It drew about a third as many complaints as the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 35; 100 is average, lower is better). The Nissan Rogue year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2012.

What are the most common 2020 Nissan Rogue problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are automatic emergency braking (60%), brakes (27%) and electrical (17%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2020 Nissan Rogue have?

1 safety recall (latest: electrical). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2020 Nissan Rogue get?

27 to 29 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2020 Nissan Rogue safe?

NHTSA gave it 4 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style, and the full table is on the advanced page.

At what mileage do 2020 Nissan Rogue problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 1,100 and 26,000 miles (1,800–43,000 km) (the middle half of the 52 complaints, of all 152 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K, 100K and 150K miles, a 2020 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2020 Nissan Rogue have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues aren't among its top 5 complaint areas.

How much does the 2020 Nissan Rogue cost in fuel a year?

About $2,200 to $2,400 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2020 Nissan Rogue have?

2.0L 4-cyl, 2.5L 4-cyl.

Is there a better Nissan Rogue year than the 2020?

Not nearby: the 2020 drew the fewest complaints per car sold of the Nissan Rogue years within 3 model years of it. Across every year, 2012 drew the fewest.

Quick answers

Is the 2023 Nissan Rogue reliable?

It's too early to be sure. So far it has drawn about twice the average car's complaints per car sold, against other 2023 cars after the same time on the road (early Lemon Index 230; 100 is average). 2023 is one of the weaker years of a model that usually draws a bit over half as many complaints as the average car. The Nissan Rogue year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2012.

What are the most common 2023 Nissan Rogue problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are engine (56%), fuel system (13%) and transmission & drivetrain (12%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2023 Nissan Rogue have?

6 safety recalls (latest: engine). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2023 Nissan Rogue get?

31 to 33 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2023 Nissan Rogue safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style, and the full table is on the advanced page.

At what mileage do 2023 Nissan Rogue problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 20,000 and 67,000 miles (32,000–108,000 km) (the middle half of the 154 complaints, of all 740 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K miles, a 2023 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 100K and 150K miles, a 2023 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2023 Nissan Rogue have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 12% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2023 Nissan Rogue cost in fuel a year?

About $1,950 to $2,050 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2023 Nissan Rogue have?

1.5L 3-cyl.

What Nissan Rogue year is better than the 2023?

Nearby, the 2021 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (about half as many complaints as the average car). Across every year, 2012 drew the fewest.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 26V080000 (engine), 25V437000 (engine) and 24V156000 (seat belts) done? It has 6 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  3. Any fuel smell, stalling or hard starts? On a hybrid or EV, any reduced-power warning or battery work?
  4. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  5. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced? (14 owners named a electrical repair bill; the middle one was $2,600.)
  6. Nissan sent dealers 113 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Vibration and/or noise from the rear of the vehicle when turning if you confirm a binding…”?

How to read this chart

How the YouTube ranks work

YouTube rank: we read 597 YouTube videos about the Nissan Rogue, 2008 to 2025, and ranked every year by how much they praise it vs. how much they talk about problems. YouTube's favorite: 2025. Old cars rank lower partly because most of their videos are repair and restoration videos. A gray dot means not enough data yet: under 10 videos, or a new year with no owner complaints to compare.

How to read these numbers

The Lemon Index counts owner complaints filed with the U.S. government (NHTSA) in a car's first 3 years, per car sold, against the average car of the same model years (100 = average, lower is better). It measures complaints, not crash safety. A complaint is an owner's report, not a confirmed defect. How it works

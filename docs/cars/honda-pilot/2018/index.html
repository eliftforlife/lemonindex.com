When problems show up

Most problems on this year showed up between 16,000 and 86,000 miles (26,000–139,000 km) (the middle half of the 104 complaints, of all 440 filed so far, that listed a mileage).

  • At 60K miles (97K km) Right in the range where most owners reported problems.
  • At 100K and 150K miles (161K and 241K km) Past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most.

By area: engine mostly 50,000–120,000 miles (80,000–193,000 km); electrical mostly 18,000–91,000 miles (29,000–146,000 km).

This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not the odds that one car will have it.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 26V365000 (body structure), 26V332000 (airbags) and 23V858000 (fuel system, gasoline) done? It has 7 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. NHTSA has an open investigation into no restart after auto start/stop engages. Has this car had that problem?
  3. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  4. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  5. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  6. Does the screen respond, and does the backup camera come on every time? (47 owners named a screen, radio & camera repair bill; the middle one was $2,000.)
  7. Honda sent dealers 140 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “The transmission end cover leaks on vehicles with a 9-speed A/T”?

What to ask the shop

  • Engine: ask whether an engine special coverage or warranty extension applies. Covered repairs can be free. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10232973: “Warranty Extension - Warranty Extension reimbursement form for engine idle stop repairs made before the warranty…”)
  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10187008: “The PCM software may misinterpret sensor inputs as a deteriorated catalytic converter”)
  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 11017320: “Torque converters may not have been manufactured to proper specifications”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

  • Engineering Request for Investigation - American Honda Motor Co., Inc

Ask a seller or mechanic if these were done. Some are free under warranty or a special program.

Quick answers

Is the 2018 Honda Pilot reliable?

Yes, it is one of the most reliable by government complaints. It drew about half as many complaints as the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 48; 100 is average, lower is better). The Honda Pilot year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2010.

What are the most common 2018 Honda Pilot problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are electrical (18%), transmission & drivetrain (18%) and engine (17%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2018 Honda Pilot have?

7 safety recalls (latest: body structure). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2018 Honda Pilot get?

21 to 23 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2018 Honda Pilot safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style, and the full table is on the advanced page.

At what mileage do 2018 Honda Pilot problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 16,000 and 86,000 miles (26,000–139,000 km) (the middle half of the 104 complaints, of all 440 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K miles, a 2018 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 100K and 150K miles, a 2018 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2018 Honda Pilot have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 18% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2018 Honda Pilot cost in fuel a year?

About $2,800 to $3,050 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2018 Honda Pilot have?

3.5L 6-cyl.

Is there a better Honda Pilot year than the 2018?

Not nearby: the 2018 drew the fewest complaints per car sold of the Honda Pilot years within 3 model years of it. Across every year, 2010 drew the fewest.

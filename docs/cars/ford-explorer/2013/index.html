What to ask the shop

  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10056136: “Ford/lincoln: diagnostic trouble code (dtc) p0420 and/or p0430, the malfunction indicator lamp (mil), on some vehicles,…”)
  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10055640: “Lincoln/ford: on some vehicles, there are no upshift, only reverse and first gear available, which is due to a software…”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2013 Ford Explorer reliable?

No, it is worse than average by government complaints. It drew about twice the average car's complaints filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 195; 100 is average, lower is better). The Ford Explorer year with the fewest complaints per car sold is 2008.

What are the most common 2013 Ford Explorer problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are engine (26%), steering (18%) and body structure (16%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2013 Ford Explorer have?

12 safety recalls (latest: body structure). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2013 Ford Explorer get?

18 to 23 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2013 Ford Explorer safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style, and the full table is on the advanced page.

At what mileage do 2013 Ford Explorer problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 45,000 and 112,000 miles (73,000–180,000 km) (the middle half of the 1,531 complaints, of all 2,237 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K and 100K miles, a 2013 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 150K miles, a 2013 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2013 Ford Explorer have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues aren't among its top 5 complaint areas.

How much does the 2013 Ford Explorer cost in fuel a year?

About $2,800 to $3,550 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2013 Ford Explorer have?

2.0L 4-cyl, 3.5L 6-cyl.

What Ford Explorer year is better than the 2013?

Nearby, the 2010 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (about three-quarters as many complaints as the average car). Across every year, 2008 drew the fewest.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 24V031000 (body structure), 21V746000 (suspension) and 21V537000 (suspension) done? It has 12 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  3. Any steering noise, wander or power-steering warning? Has the steering rack or pump been replaced?
  4. Any rust or damage underneath, or doors and hatches that don't close right?
  5. Ford sent dealers 139 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Lincoln/ford: due to seal and/or bushing wearing caused by halfshaft surface finish, on some…”?

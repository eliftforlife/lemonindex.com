What to ask the shop

  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10184781: “Some 2020-2021 Explorer/Police Interceptor Utility vehicles equipped with a 3.3L hybrid engine and built on or before…”)
  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10187917: “Some 2020-2021 Explorer vehicles equipped with a 10R60/10R80-MHT automatic transmission and built on or before…”)
  • Screen, radio & camera: ask for the radio / screen software update first; it often fixes freezes and reboots without new parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10199514: “Some 2020-2021 Explorer/Aviator/Corsair vehicles may exhibit an intermittent blue screen on the center infotainment…”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2021 Ford Explorer reliable?

About average by government complaints. It drew a bit more than the average car's complaints filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 112; 100 is average, lower is better). The Ford Explorer year with the fewest complaints per car sold is 2008.

What are the most common 2021 Ford Explorer problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are transmission & drivetrain (32%), backup camera (13%) and electrical (13%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2021 Ford Explorer have?

26 safety recalls (latest: seats). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2021 Ford Explorer get?

19 to 27 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2021 Ford Explorer safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style.

At what mileage do 2021 Ford Explorer problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 25,000 and 73,000 miles (40,000–117,000 km) (the middle half of the 109 complaints, of all 453 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K miles, a 2021 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 100K and 150K miles, a 2021 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2021 Ford Explorer have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 32% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2021 Ford Explorer cost in fuel a year?

About $2,400 to $3,400 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2021 Ford Explorer have?

2.3L 4-cyl, 3.0L 6-cyl, 3.3L 6-cyl.

What Ford Explorer year is better than the 2021?

Nearby, the 2022 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (about three-quarters as many complaints as the average car). Across every year, 2008 drew the fewest.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 26V451000 (seats), 26V402000 (transmission & drivetrain) and 26V159000 (seat belts) done? It has 26 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. NHTSA has an open investigation into burn injuries from side airbag deployment. Has this car had that problem?
  3. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  4. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  5. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records. (9 owners named a engine repair bill; the middle one was $3,500.)
  6. Ford sent dealers 297 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Intermittent no crank, inoperative remote”?

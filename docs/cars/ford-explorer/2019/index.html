When problems show up

Most problems on this year showed up between 25,000 and 80,000 miles (40,000–128,000 km) (the middle half of the 117 complaints, of all 299 filed so far, that listed a mileage).

  • At 60K miles (97K km) Right in the range where most owners reported problems.
  • At 100K and 150K miles (161K and 241K km) Past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most.

By area: body structure mostly 39,000–89,000 miles (62,000–144,000 km).

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 26V448000 (body structure), 26V101000 (suspension) and 26V012000 (equipment) done? It has 6 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any rust or damage underneath, or doors and hatches that don't close right?
  3. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  4. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  5. Any steering noise, wander or power-steering warning? Has the steering rack or pump been replaced? (203 owners named a steering repair bill; the middle one was $2,100.)
  6. Ford sent dealers 135 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Illuminated MIL with ambient air temperature”?

What to ask the shop

  • Transmission: ask whether a transmission special coverage or warranty extension applies to this car. Covered repairs can be free. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10222455: “Certain 2015-2019 MKC and 2016-2019 Explorer Vehicles Equipped With the 2.3L Engine and 6F35 Transmission -…”)
  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10181985: “In affected vehicles, the transmission torque converter may overheat in certain driving conditions”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

Quick answers

Is the 2019 Ford Explorer reliable?

Yes, it is one of the most reliable by government complaints. It drew about a third as many complaints as the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 32; 100 is average, lower is better). 2019 is one of the better years of a model that draws 1.8× the average car's complaints.

What are the most common 2019 Ford Explorer problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are body structure (31%), engine (15%) and electrical (15%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2019 Ford Explorer have?

6 safety recalls (latest: body structure). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2019 Ford Explorer get?

18 to 22 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2019 Ford Explorer safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style, and the full table is on the advanced page.

At what mileage do 2019 Ford Explorer problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 25,000 and 80,000 miles (40,000–128,000 km) (the middle half of the 117 complaints, of all 299 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K miles, a 2019 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 100K and 150K miles, a 2019 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2019 Ford Explorer have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues aren't among its top 5 complaint areas.

How much does the 2019 Ford Explorer cost in fuel a year?

About $2,900 to $3,550 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2019 Ford Explorer have?

2.3L 4-cyl, 3.5L 6-cyl.

Is there a better Ford Explorer year than the 2019?

Not nearby: the 2019 drew the fewest complaints per car sold of the Ford Explorer years within 3 model years of it.

When problems show up

Most problems on this year showed up between 53,000 and 108,000 miles (the middle half of the 2,521 complaints that listed a mileage).

  • At 60K and 100K miles Right in the range where most owners reported problems.
  • At 150K miles Past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most.

By area: transmission & drivetrain mostly 57,000–110,000 miles; body structure mostly 41,000–98,000 miles.

This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not the odds that one car will have it.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 09V399000 (speed control), 09E025000 (lights) and 09E012000 (lights) done? It has 10 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  3. Any rust or damage underneath, or doors and hatches that don't close right?
  4. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  5. Ford sent dealers 258 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “On some vehicles, including sport and sport trac, the front axle leaks fluid from the vent tube”?

What to ask the shop

  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 620163: “Some vehicles built 11/13/2000 through 3/8/2001 equipped with a 5r55w transmission may exhibit a transmission control…”)
  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10003844: “Driveability conditions due to calibration vehicles equipped with the 4.0l sohc engine”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

Quick answers

Is the 2002 Ford Explorer reliable?

No, it is one of the worst by government complaints. It drew 2.1× the average car's complaints filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 207; 100 is average, lower is better). The Ford Explorer year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2019.

What are the most common 2002 Ford Explorer problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are transmission & drivetrain (36%), body structure (21%) and engine (8%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2002 Ford Explorer have?

10 safety recalls (latest: speed control). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2002 Ford Explorer get?

14 to 17 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

At what mileage do 2002 Ford Explorer problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 53,000 and 108,000 miles (the middle half of the 2,521 complaints that listed a mileage). At 60K and 100K miles, a 2002 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 150K miles, a 2002 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2002 Ford Explorer have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 36% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2002 Ford Explorer cost in fuel a year?

About $3800 to $4600 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2002 Ford Explorer have?

4.0L 6-cyl, 4.6L 8-cyl.

What Ford Explorer year is better than the 2002?

Nearby, the 2005 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (1.9× fewer complaints than average). Across every year, 2019 drew the fewest.

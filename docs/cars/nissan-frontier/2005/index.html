What to ask the shop

  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10033344: “Nissan: dtc p0420 (tw catalyst sys-bi) stored in the ecm”)
  • Transmission: ask whether the latest transmission software update was done, and ask for it before paying for parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10192821: “SERVICE INFORMATION Replacement 5-speed rear wheel drive Automatic Transmission (A/T) assemblies and replacement…”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2005 Nissan Frontier reliable?

About average by government complaints. It drew about the average number of complaints filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 91; 100 is average, lower is better). The Nissan Frontier year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2019.

What are the most common 2005 Nissan Frontier problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are fuel system, gasoline (46%), transmission & drivetrain (20%) and body structure (17%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2005 Nissan Frontier have?

6 safety recalls (latest: engine). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2005 Nissan Frontier get?

16 to 21 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

At what mileage do 2005 Nissan Frontier problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 62,000 and 115,000 miles (100,000–185,000 km) (the middle half of the 476 complaints, of all 532 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K miles, a 2005 is still under the range where most problems showed up, so they may be ahead of it. At 100K miles, a 2005 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 150K miles, a 2005 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2005 Nissan Frontier have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 20% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2005 Nissan Frontier cost in fuel a year?

About $3,050 to $4,000 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2005 Nissan Frontier have?

2.5L 4-cyl, 4.0L 6-cyl.

What Nissan Frontier year is better than the 2005?

Nearby, the 2008 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (about half as many complaints as the average car). Across every year, 2019 drew the fewest.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 10V517000 (engine), 10E019000 (suspension) and 08V690000 (airbags) done? It has 6 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any fuel smell, stalling or hard starts? On a hybrid or EV, any reduced-power warning or battery work?
  3. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  4. Any rust or damage underneath, or doors and hatches that don't close right?
  5. Any overheating, coolant smell or leaks? Ask whether the water pump or radiator was replaced. (56 owners named a cooling repair bill; the middle one was $4,800.)
  6. Nissan sent dealers 109 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Rear axle seal leaks”?

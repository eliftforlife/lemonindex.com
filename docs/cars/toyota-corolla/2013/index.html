When problems show up

Most problems on this year showed up between 6,200 and 69,000 miles (10,000–111,000 km) (the middle half of the 138 complaints, of all 201 filed so far, that listed a mileage).

  • At 60K miles (97K km) Right in the range where most owners reported problems.
  • At 100K and 150K miles (161K and 241K km) Past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most.

By area: airbags mostly 14,000–72,000 miles (22,000–116,000 km); steering mostly 100–36,000 miles (160–59,000 km).

This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not the odds that one car will have it.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 20V024000 (airbags), 19V741000 (airbags) and 19V005000 (airbags) done? It has 5 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. NHTSA has an open investigation into desiccated air bag inflator rupture. Has this car had that problem?
  3. Is the airbag light off, and were any airbag recalls done?
  4. Any steering noise, wander or power-steering warning? Has the steering rack or pump been replaced?
  5. Does it accelerate and hold speed smoothly, with no surging or stalling?
  6. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records. (11 owners named a engine repair bill; the middle one was $2,000.)
  7. Toyota sent dealers 353 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Some customers have experienced amber colored oil like substance found on floor mats”?

Quick answers

Is the 2013 Toyota Corolla reliable?

Yes, it is one of the most reliable by government complaints. It drew about a quarter as many complaints as the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 27; 100 is average, lower is better). The Toyota Corolla year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2012.

What are the most common 2013 Toyota Corolla problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are airbags (28%), steering (27%) and speed control (14%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2013 Toyota Corolla have?

5 safety recalls (latest: airbags). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2013 Toyota Corolla get?

29 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2013 Toyota Corolla safe?

NHTSA gave it 4 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style, and the full table is on the advanced page.

At what mileage do 2013 Toyota Corolla problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 6,200 and 69,000 miles (10,000–111,000 km) (the middle half of the 138 complaints, of all 201 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K miles, a 2013 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 100K and 150K miles, a 2013 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2013 Toyota Corolla have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues aren't among its top 5 complaint areas.

How much does the 2013 Toyota Corolla cost in fuel a year?

About $2,200 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2013 Toyota Corolla have?

1.8L 4-cyl.

What Toyota Corolla year is better than the 2013?

Nearby, the 2012 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (about a fifth as many complaints as the average car).

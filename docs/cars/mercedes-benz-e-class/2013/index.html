Quick answers

Is the 2013 Mercedes-Benz E-Class reliable?

Yes, it is one of the most reliable by government complaints. It drew 4.2× fewer complaints than average filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 24; 100 is average, lower is better). The best Mercedes-Benz E-Class year by this measure is 2011.

What are the most common 2013 Mercedes-Benz E-Class problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are speed control (30%), wheels (20%) and engine (15%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2013 Mercedes-Benz E-Class have?

6 safety recalls (latest: airbags). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2013 Mercedes-Benz E-Class get?

22 to 23 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

At what mileage do 2013 Mercedes-Benz E-Class problems start?

Half of the owners who gave a mileage reported the problem between 20,900 and 80,000 miles. The middle report was at 44,100 miles, from 75 complaints.

Does the 2013 Mercedes-Benz E-Class have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues aren't among its top 5 complaint areas.

How much does the 2013 Mercedes-Benz E-Class cost in fuel a year?

About $3450 to $3600 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2013 Mercedes-Benz E-Class have?

3.5L 6-cyl.

What Mercedes-Benz E-Class year is better than the 2013?

By owner complaints per car sold, the best recent years are 2011, 2012 and 2013.

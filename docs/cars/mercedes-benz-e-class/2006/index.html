How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Quick answers

Is the 2006 Mercedes-Benz E-Class reliable?

Yes, better than average by government complaints. It drew somewhat fewer complaints than the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Complaint Score 83; 100 is average, lower is better). The Mercedes-Benz E-Class year with the fewest complaints per car sold since 2010 is 2011.

What are the most common 2006 Mercedes-Benz E-Class problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are transmission & drivetrain (21%), airbags (17%) and electrical (14%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2006 Mercedes-Benz E-Class have?

4 safety recalls (latest: visibility). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2006 Mercedes-Benz E-Class get?

16 to 27 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

At what mileage do 2006 Mercedes-Benz E-Class problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 60,000 and 120,000 miles (97,000–193,000 km) (the middle half of the 421 complaints, of all 503 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K and 100K miles, a 2006 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 150K miles, a 2006 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2006 Mercedes-Benz E-Class have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 21% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2006 Mercedes-Benz E-Class cost in fuel a year?

About $3,500 to $5,000 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2006 Mercedes-Benz E-Class have?

3.2L 6-cyl, 3.5L 6-cyl, 5.0L 8-cyl.

What Mercedes-Benz E-Class year is better than the 2006?

Nearby, the 2004 drew fewer owner complaints per car sold (about half as many complaints as the average car). Across every year, 2011 drew the fewest.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 24V874000 (visibility), 22V954000 (visibility) and 21V196000 (visibility) done? It has 4 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  3. Is the airbag light off, and were any airbag recalls done?
  4. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  5. Any fuel smell, stalling or hard starts? On a hybrid or EV, any reduced-power warning or battery work? (58 owners named a fuel system repair bill; the middle one was $2,400.)
  6. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records. (23 owners named a engine repair bill; the middle one was $5,000.)
  7. Mercedes-Benz sent dealers 136 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Dirt can collect in the rear sleeves on the front axle carrier”?

Questions

Quick answers

Is the 2019 Ford F-150 reliable?

We can't score it: Ford reports F-150 and Super Duty sales together as one F-Series number (there is no F-150-only sales figure), so there is no per-car rate. Owners filed 252 complaints with NHTSA in its first 3 years.

Does this include the 2019 F-250 and F-350?

No. These are the 252 complaints NHTSA files as F-150 or F-150 Hybrid. The 2019 Super Duty drew 1,299 of its own in its first 3 years; they are on its own page.

What are the most common 2019 Ford F-150 problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are transmission & drivetrain (37%), engine (22%) and body structure (8%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2019 Ford F-150 have?

9 safety recalls (latest: lights). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2019 Ford F-150 get?

18 to 24 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2019 Ford F-150 safe?

NHTSA gave it 5 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style, and the full table is on the advanced page.

At what mileage do 2019 Ford F-150 problems show up?

Most problems on this year showed up between 25,000 and 92,000 miles (40,000–148,000 km) (the middle half of the 435 complaints, of all 1,351 filed so far, that listed a mileage). At 60K miles, a 2019 is right in the range where most owners reported problems. At 100K and 150K miles, a 2019 is past the range where most problems showed up: it has likely had them or dodged them, so repair records matter most. This is when owners who had a problem reported it, not a prediction for one car.

Does the 2019 Ford F-150 have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 37% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2019 Ford F-150 cost in fuel a year?

About $3,050 to $4,300 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2019 Ford F-150 have?

2.7L 6-cyl, 3.0L 6-cyl, 3.3L 6-cyl, 3.5L 6-cyl, 5.0L 8-cyl.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 26V373000 (lights), 25V442000 (back over prevention) and 24V852000 (equipment) done? It has 9 recalls in all. Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  3. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  4. Any rust or damage underneath, or doors and hatches that don't close right?
  5. Ford sent dealers 332 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Some 2017-2019 F-150, 2018-2019 Expedition/Navigator/Mustang, 2019 Ranger vehicles equipped…”?

How to read this chart

Newest year first. Complaints = F-150 owner complaints filed with NHTSA in each model year's first 3 years. It is a count, not a rate: years that sold more draw more, and Ford doesn't report F-150 sales on their own, so we can't turn it into a per-truck score. #1, #2… = that year's YouTube rank among the 50 F-150 years with enough videos (#1 = the year owners and reviewers talk about most positively). It is not a reliability rank.

How the YouTube ranks work

YouTube rank: we read 1,751 YouTube videos about the Ford F-Series, 1975 to 2026, and ranked every year by how much they praise it vs. how much they talk about problems. YouTube's favorite: 2024. Old cars rank lower partly because most of their videos are repair and restoration videos. A gray dot means not enough data yet: under 10 videos, or a new year with no owner complaints to compare.

How to read these numbers

The Lemon Index counts owner complaints filed with the U.S. government (NHTSA) in a car's first 3 years, per car sold, against the average car of the same model years (100 = average, lower is better). It measures complaints, not crash safety. A complaint is an owner's report, not a confirmed defect. How it works

What to ask the shop

  • Transmission: ask whether a transmission special coverage or warranty extension applies to this car. Covered repairs can be free. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10234452: “Customer Satisfaction Notification ZD3 9-Speed Transmission”)
  • Engine: ask whether the engine computer (PCM) update was done before chasing parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10214633: “Flash: Powertrain Control Module (PCM) Updates This bulletin involves reprogramming the PCM with the latest available…”)
  • Screen, radio & camera: ask for the radio / screen software update first; it often fixes freezes and reboots without new parts. (automaker note to dealers, NHTSA document 10240004: “SOS, Assistance Calls Not Successful After Radio Replacement or Radio Software Update”)

Each range is the middle half of the bills owners wrote down (one repair, parts and labor as they said it), not a yearly or total cost. Owners who complain often had expensive repairs, so typical bills can be lower. Prices depend on the shop, the miles and where you live.

How these estimates work

Labor hours and parts prices are AI estimates (written by Claude for each generation, for an independent shop with quality aftermarket parts), checked against the repair bills owners reported. Confidence says how well owner bills back the number: High = many owner bills that agree, Medium = some owner bills, Low = an estimate with few or no owner bills behind it. With too little to go on we show no number. Where this generation's owner bills are strong (or far from the estimate), what owners paid is the main number: those are mostly dealer bills that owners named in complaints to NHTSA, so they often run higher than an independent shop. Not a quote: prices vary by shop, region, parts and miles.

Only the repair, the price and the first 3 digits of your ZIP are kept. No name or email. One per repair per car.

Quick answers

Is the 2022 Jeep Cherokee reliable?

Yes, it is one of the most reliable by government complaints. It drew about half as many complaints as the average car filed with NHTSA in its first 3 years, per car sold (Lemon Index 46; 100 is average, lower is better). 2022 is one of the better years of a model that draws 3.1× the average car's complaints.

What are the most common 2022 Jeep Cherokee problems?

In owner complaints to the government, the parts named most are transmission & drivetrain (52%), engine (23%) and electrical (19%). That is the share of complaints, not the share of cars that break.

How many recalls does the 2022 Jeep Cherokee have?

3 safety recalls (latest: transmission & drivetrain). Recall repairs are free at any dealer; check a specific car's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

What gas mileage does the 2022 Jeep Cherokee get?

21 to 26 mpg combined, depending on the engine (EPA estimate).

Is the 2022 Jeep Cherokee safe?

NHTSA gave it 4 out of 5 stars overall in its best tested version. Ratings differ by body style.

Does the 2022 Jeep Cherokee have transmission problems?

Transmission and drivetrain issues make up 52% of its owner complaints.

How much does the 2022 Jeep Cherokee cost in fuel a year?

About $2,450 to $3,050 a year, by the EPA's current estimate.

What engines does the 2022 Jeep Cherokee have?

2.0L 4-cyl, 2.4L 4-cyl, 3.2L 6-cyl.

Is there a better Jeep Cherokee year than the 2022?

Not nearby: the 2022 drew the fewest complaints per car sold of the Jeep Cherokee years within 3 model years of it.

Questions to ask the seller

  1. Were recalls 26V290000 (transmission & drivetrain), 24V516000 (airbags) and 24V239000 (lights) done? Ask for the dealer printout, or check the VIN.
  2. Any slipping, hard or late shifts, or a transmission warning light? Ask for transmission fluid service records.
  3. Any oil burning, leaks or check-engine lights? Ask for oil-change records.
  4. Do all the lights, screens and power features work? Has the battery, alternator or any wiring been replaced?
  5. Jeep sent dealers 169 repair notes for this car. Was this one done: “Battery Drain Concern, Dual USB Port Normal Operational Draw, Extended Illumination Or…”?

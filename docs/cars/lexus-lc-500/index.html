Known issues

  • High-pressure fuel pump recallRecall

    On some 2018 LC 500s (and RC F, GS F), a damper in one of the two high-pressure fuel pumps could crack the pump cover and leak fuel.

    Fix: Dealer replaces the high-pressure fuel pumps for free (NHTSA 18V-107).

  • In-tank fuel pump recallRecall

    The low-pressure fuel pump in the tank of some 2018–2020 LC 500 and LC 500h cars can fail, which can stall the engine while driving.

    Fix: Dealer fits an improved fuel pump for free (NHTSA 20V-012 and 20V-682).

    1 owner complaint to NHTSA mention it (2018–)
  • Water pump seepage vs. real leakMaker bulletin

    A Lexus bulletin covering 2018–2021 LC 500, LC 500 convertible and LC 500h says dry coolant residue at the pump is normal; wet residue or drips is a real leak.

    Fix: Replace the water pump only for an active, wet leak.

  • Valley plate coolant leakOwner-reported

    Sealant under the valley plate can fail with heat cycles and leak coolant. Guides tie it mainly to older IS F engines past about 8 years and 80,000 miles, not the LC.

    Fix: Reseal the valley plate; Jalopnik puts it at about $1,500 to over $3,000.

Quick answers

How fast is the Lexus LC 500 coupe?

The URZ100 LC 500 coupe makes 471 hp and 398 lb-ft. Car and Driver measured 0–60 mph in 4.6 seconds. Top speed: 168 mph (Lexus top track speed).

What engine does the Lexus LC 500 have?

The URZ100 LC 500 (2018–) uses the Lexus 2UR-GSE, a 5.0-liter V-8, naturally aspirated.

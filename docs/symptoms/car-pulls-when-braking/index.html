Car pulls to one side when braking.

Brakes · owner complaints1,481
What it usually means

Pulling to one side under braking usually means one side is braking harder than the other.

How serious

Fix soon. Get it checked soon, and leave more room to stop.

Where a shop starts

A sticking or seized caliper. Then: a collapsed brake hose on one side; uneven or contaminated brake pads.

Owners describe this in 1,481 complaints to the U.S. government, most often on the Toyota Sequoia.

Common causes of car pulls to one side when braking
  • A sticking or seized caliper
  • A collapsed brake hose on one side
  • Uneven or contaminated brake pads
  • Worn suspension bushings or ball joints
  • Uneven tire pressure

General causes, not a diagnosis. A shop should confirm the cause with tests before replacing parts.

Is it safe to drive if your car pulls to one side when braking?

Fix soon. Get it checked soon, and leave more room to stop.

Get help right away if the pull is strong; you smell burning from one wheel.

Questions

What causes car pulls to one side when braking?

Pulling to one side under braking usually means one side is braking harder than the other. Common causes: a sticking or seized caliper; a collapsed brake hose on one side; uneven or contaminated brake pads; worn suspension bushings or ball joints; uneven tire pressure.

Is it safe to drive if your car pulls to one side when braking?

Fix soon. Get it checked soon, and leave more room to stop.

Which cars have this problem most?

In complaints to NHTSA, owners describe it most on the Toyota Sequoia, Mercedes-Benz C-Class and Ram pickup. These are raw counts, so popular models rank higher; complaints are owner reports, not proof of a defect.

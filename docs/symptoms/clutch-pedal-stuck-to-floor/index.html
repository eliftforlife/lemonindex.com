Clutch pedal stuck to the floor.

Transmission · owner complaints1,006
What it usually means

A clutch pedal that stays down or feels soft usually means the clutch hydraulics have lost pressure.

How serious

Fix soon. You may not be able to shift. Have it towed or checked.

Where a shop starts

A leaking clutch master or slave cylinder. Then: low or leaking clutch fluid; a broken clutch cable.

Owners describe this in 1,006 complaints to the U.S. government, most often on the Ford Focus.

Common causes of clutch pedal stuck to the floor
  • A leaking clutch master or slave cylinder
  • Low or leaking clutch fluid
  • A broken clutch cable
  • A broken pressure plate spring
  • Air in the clutch line

General causes, not a diagnosis. A shop should confirm the cause with tests before replacing parts.

Is it safe to drive if the clutch pedal sticks to the floor?

Fix soon. You may not be able to shift. Have it towed or checked.

Get help right away if you can’t get it out of gear.

Questions

What causes clutch pedal stuck to the floor?

A clutch pedal that stays down or feels soft usually means the clutch hydraulics have lost pressure. Common causes: a leaking clutch master or slave cylinder; low or leaking clutch fluid; a broken clutch cable; a broken pressure plate spring; air in the clutch line.

Is it safe to drive if the clutch pedal sticks to the floor?

Fix soon. You may not be able to shift. Have it towed or checked.

Which cars have this problem most?

In complaints to NHTSA, owners describe it most on the Ford Focus, Nissan Altima and Volkswagen Jetta. These are raw counts, so popular models rank higher; complaints are owner reports, not proof of a defect.

How much does it cost to fix?

In 37 repair bills owners named in complaints describing it, the middle half ran $1,700 to $4,000. The right repair depends on the cause.

Grinding noise when braking.

Brakes · owner complaints3,595
What it usually means

Grinding when you brake usually means the pads are worn down and metal is touching the rotor.

How serious

Fix soon. Get it checked soon. Metal-on-metal braking stops worse and ruins rotors quickly.

The details

Common causes of grinding noise when braking
  • Brake pads worn down to the backing plate
  • A stone or debris caught between the pad and rotor
  • A seized caliper or a pad stuck against the rotor
  • Rust on the rotors after the car has sat (this usually clears in a few stops)
  • A worn wheel bearing

General causes, not a diagnosis. A shop should confirm the cause with tests before replacing parts.

Is it safe to drive with grinding brakes?

Fix soon. Get it checked soon. Metal-on-metal braking stops worse and ruins rotors quickly.

Get help right away if the noise is constant and loud; the car takes longer to stop; the brake warning light is on.

Of the 3,595 complaints describing it, 4% mention a crash, 0.8% say the car was towed and 1% report an injury.

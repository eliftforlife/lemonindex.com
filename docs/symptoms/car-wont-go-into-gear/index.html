Car won't go into gear.

Transmission · owner complaints12,236
What it usually means

A car that won’t shift into gear, or is slow to engage, usually has a transmission or shifter problem.

How serious

Fix soon. Don’t force it. If it won’t engage, have it towed.

Where a shop starts

Low transmission fluid. Then: a broken shifter cable or linkage; a faulty brake switch or shift interlock.

Owners describe this in 12,236 complaints to the U.S. government, most often on the Ford F-Series.

The details

Common causes of car won't go into gear
  • Low transmission fluid
  • A broken shifter cable or linkage
  • A faulty brake switch or shift interlock
  • A transmission control fault
  • Internal transmission wear

General causes, not a diagnosis. A shop should confirm the cause with tests before replacing parts.

Is it safe to drive if your car won't go into gear?

Fix soon. Don’t force it. If it won’t engage, have it towed.

Get help right away if it slips out of gear while driving.

Of the 12,236 complaints describing it, 2% mention a crash, 3% say the car was towed, 0.7% mention a fire and 0.9% report an injury.

Quick answers

What causes car won't go into gear?

A car that won’t shift into gear, or is slow to engage, usually has a transmission or shifter problem. Common causes: low transmission fluid; a broken shifter cable or linkage; a faulty brake switch or shift interlock; a transmission control fault; internal transmission wear.

Is it safe to drive if your car won't go into gear?

Fix soon. Don’t force it. If it won’t engage, have it towed.

Which cars have this problem most?

In complaints to NHTSA, owners describe it most on the Ford F-Series, Ford Focus and Chrysler 300. These are raw counts, so popular models rank higher; complaints are owner reports, not proof of a defect.

How much does it cost to fix?

In 329 repair bills owners named in complaints describing it, the middle half ran $2,200 to $6,000. The right repair depends on the cause.

Check engine light flashing.

Engine · owner complaints3,859
What it usually means

A flashing check engine light usually means a misfire bad enough to damage the catalytic converter.

How serious

Stop and get help. Ease off and get it to a shop now. Avoid hard acceleration and long drives.

Where a shop starts

An engine misfire (spark plugs, coils or injectors). Then: a failing ignition coil; a fuel delivery problem.

Common causes of check engine light flashing
  • An engine misfire (spark plugs, coils or injectors)
  • A failing ignition coil
  • A fuel delivery problem
  • Low compression in a cylinder
  • A vacuum leak

General causes, not a diagnosis. A shop should confirm the cause with tests before replacing parts.

Is it safe to drive with the check engine light flashing?

Stop and get help. Ease off and get it to a shop now. Avoid hard acceleration and long drives.

Get help right away if you notice it at all.

Of the 3,859 complaints describing it, 0.5% mention a crash, 3% say the car was towed and 2% mention a fire.

Questions

What causes check engine light flashing?

A flashing check engine light usually means a misfire bad enough to damage the catalytic converter. Common causes: an engine misfire (spark plugs, coils or injectors); a failing ignition coil; a fuel delivery problem; low compression in a cylinder; a vacuum leak.

Is it safe to drive with the check engine light flashing?

Stop and get help. Ease off and get it to a shop now. Avoid hard acceleration and long drives.

Which cars have this problem most?

In complaints to NHTSA, owners describe it most on the Hyundai Sonata, Ford F-Series and Kia Optima. These are raw counts, so popular models rank higher; complaints are owner reports, not proof of a defect.

How much does it cost to fix?

In 166 repair bills owners named in complaints describing it, the middle half ran $1,700 to $8,500. The right repair depends on the cause.

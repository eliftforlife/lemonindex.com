The details
  1. Complaints: the full NHTSA owner-complaint file (1,607,537 vehicle complaints, 1995 to October 2026).
  2. Sales: U.S. sales per model per year from automakers' published figures. Tesla doesn't report by model, so we use published estimates and say so.
  3. Same window for every car: a complaint counts only if it was filed within 3 years of the model year.
  4. Rate: complaints per 100,000 cars sold, matched model year to sales year. When a new model's first cars sold the calendar year before its model year, those sales are counted with the first model year.
  5. Index: each car's rate against the average car of the same model years.

What it doesn't measure

A complaint is a safety complaint filed with the government, not a repair record. Fleet and rental-heavy models have fewer owners filing. Luxury lessees often go straight to the dealer. Some owners file more readily than others. Read it as early-life safety complaints, not total cost of ownership.

Also on every car page

  • Recalls: every NHTSA safety recall campaign for that model year (NHTSA recalls API).
  • Crash tests: NHTSA 5-star safety ratings for each body style tested (model years 2011 on).
  • Fuel economy: EPA combined mpg and yearly fuel cost from fueleconomy.gov, across the versions sold that year.
  • When problems show up: the odometer reading owners gave in their complaints. Unlike the score, this uses every complaint for the model year, because high-mileage failures come years later. Readings of 0 or over 400,000 miles are left out.
  • How serious: complaints in which the owner reported a crash, a fire, an injury, a death or a tow. Owners report these; NHTSA doesn't confirm the cause.

What people ask

Is it per car sold?

Yes. Every score divides a car's complaints by how many were sold in the U.S. So Ford outselling Chevy doesn't make Ford look worse; a car only scores badly if it drew more complaints for each car on the road.

Isn't it just owners who skip maintenance?

We only count complaints filed in a car's first 3 years, when it's nearly new and usually still under the factory warranty. That points at problems the car left the factory with, not a 15-year-old car that missed its oil changes.

Does it tell me how long a car will last?

No. It measures early complaints, not lifespan. A truck can draw a lot of complaints in its first years and still run past 300,000 miles. Use it to see which years had problems, then check the car's own history.

Why so few complaints for a problem everyone knows about?

Most owners never file with the government; they just go to a mechanic. So the counts are a small slice of real failures. That's fine for comparing cars, because every car is measured the same way, but don't read a count as the number of cars that broke.

Can you split it by engine or trim?

The government files list make, model and year, not the engine. Where the model name gives the engine away (BMW files the 328i and 335i separately, for example), we can split by it. Otherwise a score covers every engine sold that year.

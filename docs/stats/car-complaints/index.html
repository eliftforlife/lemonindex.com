Key facts
  • The 2017 Chevrolet Volt drew the most owner complaints per car sold of any model year from 2015 to 2022: a Lemon Index of 856, about 8.6 times the average car of that year.
  • The brand with the fewest complaints per car sold is Lexus (Lemon Index 30). The most: Tesla (322). 100 is the average car.
  • Electrical is the most common problem area: 18% of complaint mentions, then engine (17%) and transmission & drivetrain (16%).
  • Electric cars drew 213 complaints per 100,000 sold, against 50 for other cars of the same model years (2021–2024): about 4.3 times as many. Without driver-assist complaints, 2.7 times.
  • Only 35% of the complaints filed so far about 2010–2022 model years of the 130 models we rank came in within 3 years of the model year. The other 65% came later.
  • Of the 2010–2022 model years, 2022 drew the most complaints per car sold (127 per 100,000 in their first 3 years) and 2019 the fewest (92).
  • The most reliable popular model is the Toyota 4Runner (Lemon Index 26). The least: the Tesla Model S (430).

Why our totals differ

Each number counts a different slice of the same government file:

  • 1,607,537: every vehicle complaint in NHTSA's file, 1995 to October 2026, all makes (the study and method pages).
  • 929,012: every complaint about the 260 models we track, model years 2000 to 2025, filed at any time (the statistics page and the “How serious” counts on year pages).
  • 337,224: the same models and years, counting only complaints filed in each car's first 3 years (the complaint count on each year page and the by-year CSV).
  • 176,126: first-3-year complaints for the 130 models in the study, model years 2010 to 2022 (the home page). These set the main Lemon Index.
  • 827,013: every complaint filed at any time for the model years in the transmission CSV (its all_complaints column), so it runs larger than the first-3-year counts.

EV scores use their own window: model years 2021–2024, complaints filed by the end of the year after the model year.

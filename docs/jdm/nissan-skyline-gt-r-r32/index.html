Skyline GT-R R32 (BNR32) · 1989–1994

276horsepower
156mph top speed

2.6-liter twin-turbo RB26DETT, 280 PS (276 hp), 5-speed, all-wheel drive. Built for Japan; 100 went to Australia.

Why it's famous

Godzilla Name Australia’s Wheels magazine nicknamed the R32…
  • GodzillaName

    Australia’s Wheels magazine nicknamed the R32 GT-R “Godzilla” in its July 1989 issue. Autosport says the name stuck because the car smashed everything in its path on Australian tracks.

  • 29 wins from 29 races in JapanRacing

    The R32 was built to win Group A touring car races. In the Japanese Touring Car Championship it won all 29 races it entered from 1990 to 1993, and took the title every year.

  • Bathurst 1991 and 1992Racing

    Jim Richards and Mark Skaife won the Bathurst 1000 in an R32 GT-R in 1991 and again in 1992. The 1992 win came after a crash in heavy rain stopped the race, and the crowd booed them. New rules then all but banned the car.

  • Initial DAnime

    In Initial D, Takeshi Nakazato drives an R32 GT-R. Car Throttle calls it one of the most menacing cars in the series, though it is only lightly modified: a NISMO front bumper, Work Meister wheels and a custom exhaust.

Never sold new in the US. Never sold new in the US. Each car becomes legal to import under the 25-year rule once it is 25 years old by its month of build. R32 GT-Rs were built from August 1989 to November 1994, so every one has qualified since late 2019.

Known issues: Nissan RB26DETT

Oil pump drive failure · Ceramic turbine wheels
  • Oil pump drive failureOutlet report

    Wikipedia says early R32 engines had the crank surface that drives the oil pump machined too small, which can make the pump fail at high rpm. PistonHeads calls failing oil pump drives a persistent RB26 problem that ends in terminal engine damage, and says to look for about 2 bar of oil pressure at idle and 4 bar higher up.

    Fix: Later engines got a wider oil pump drive (Wikipedia says from the Series 3 R32; PistonHeads says from February 1993). Aftermarket collars and spline-drive kits also fix it.

  • Ceramic turbine wheelsOutlet report

    The stock turbos run about 0.8 bar. PistonHeads warns that pushing them past about 1.0 bar can make their ceramic wheels fail and wreck the engine, and that neglect or poor upgrades can make the turbos break apart.

  • Coil packs and misfiresOutlet report

    PistonHeads says misfires usually come from a failing coil pack or a bad plug, and the coil loom can cause trouble too. Replacements are cheap.

MR2 Turbo (US) SW20 (W20) · 1991–1995

200horsepower
6.30–60 mph, sec

Mid-engine, rear-drive. 2.0-liter 3S-GTE turbo with 200 hp in the US Turbo; 2.2-liter 5S-FE in the base car. US 1991–1995; Japan built it until August 1999.

Why it's famous

  • The poor man’s FerrariName

    With its mid-engine layout and looks often compared to the Ferrari 348, the SW20 was nicknamed the baby Ferrari or poor man’s Ferrari.

  • Snap oversteerName

    The SW20 is known for snap oversteer, and CarThrottle says it was called the widow maker. For 1993 Toyota revised the rear suspension, with longer rear toe links and wider 15-inch tires, to cut rear-end breakaway in hard corners.

  • JGTC GT300 record seasonRacing

    In 1998 the #25 Team Taisan Jr. with Tsuchiya MR2 won the Japanese GT Championship’s GT300 drivers’ and teams’ titles, winning a record five of the six races held. An MR2 won both GT300 titles again in 1999.

  • A tuner’s carTuning

    The 3S-GTE takes more boost well. CarThrottle says the stock turbo can give roughly 280 to 300 bhp on a safe 1 bar of boost, and a fully built engine about 350 to 400 bhp.

Sold new in the US. Sold new in the US for model years 1991 to 1995, as the non-turbo MR2 (2.2-liter 5S-FE) and the MR2 Turbo (2.0-liter 3S-GTE). Canadian sales ended after 1993. Japan kept building it until August 1999.

Known issues

  • Turbo heat and oil cokingOutlet report

    CarThrottle calls the turbo a major sore point: engine-bay heat shortens its life, and non-synthetic oil can coke the turbo bearing. Its buyer’s guides say to check for blue or white smoke at start-up and under boost, and smoke from the dipstick tube.

    Fix: Use synthetic oil and idle about three minutes after hard driving, as Toyota recommends.

  • Cooling system and overheatingOutlet report

    CarThrottle says the long cooling system is hard to bleed, and trapped air can cause overheating and a blown head gasket. A coolant hose near the exhaust manifold, nicknamed the hose from hell, can burst around 100,000 miles, and overheating on boost can point to a cracked block.

    Fix: Have the coolant flushed and bled by someone who knows the car.

  • Oil leaksOutlet report

    CarThrottle names the cam cover, distributor O-rings and head gasket as common oil leaks, and says a leak can kill the alternator, which is hard to reach in the tight engine bay.

  • Catalyst in the downpipeOutlet report

    On the MR2 Turbo the catalyst is built into the turbo downpipe, and CarThrottle says long high-speed running can damage it.

Quick answers

How fast is the Toyota MR2 Turbo (US)?

The SW20 (W20) MR2 Turbo (US) makes 200 hp and 200 lb-ft. Car and Driver (1992) measured 0–60 mph in 6.3 seconds. Top speed: 147 mph (Toyota figure, per CarThrottle).

What engine does the Toyota MR2 (SW20) have?

The SW20 (W20) MR2 (1991–1995) uses the Toyota 3S-GTE, a 2.0-liter inline-4, DOHC 16-valve, turbocharged and intercooled.

Why is the MR2 famous?

The poor man’s Ferrari: With its mid-engine layout and looks often compared to the Ferrari 348, the SW20 was nicknamed the baby Ferrari or poor man’s Ferrari. Snap oversteer: The SW20 is known for snap oversteer, and CarThrottle says it was called the widow maker. For 1993 Toyota revised the rear suspension, with longer rear toe links and wider 15-inch tires, to cut rear-end breakaway in hard corners. JGTC GT300 record season: In 1998 the #25 Team Taisan Jr. with Tsuchiya MR2 won the Japanese GT Championship’s GT300 drivers’ and teams’ titles, winning a record five of the six races held. An MR2 won both GT300 titles again in 1999.

Was the Toyota MR2 (SW20) sold in the US?

Yes. Sold new in the US for model years 1991 to 1995, as the non-turbo MR2 (2.2-liter 5S-FE) and the MR2 Turbo (2.0-liter 3S-GTE). Canadian sales ended after 1993. Japan kept building it until August 1999.

Known issues

  • Crank walkOwner-reported (NHTSA)

    Twelve NHTSA complaints on 1995-1998 Eclipses describe crank walk, where the crankshaft moves back and forth and wears the thrust bearing. Owners report failed crank sensors, stalling and engine replacement, and some say mechanics called it common.

    11 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (1995–1999)
  • Transfer case lockup recallRecall

    On all-wheel-drive Eclipses from 1990-1998, the transfer case can lock up from too little oil, which can cause a loss of control. NHTSA also has 39 owner complaints about transfer case leaks or lockups on 1995-1999 cars.

    Fix: Dealer checks the oil level and leaks, and replaces a worn transfer case (NHTSA 98V-069).

    39 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (1995–1999)
  • Timing belt failureOwner-reported (NHTSA)

    Ten complaints on 1995-1999 Eclipses describe a timing belt or tensioner failing. Several say it wrecked the valves or the top half of the engine. The complaints do not always say which engine the car had.

    9 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (1995–1999)

Quick answers

How fast is the Mitsubishi Eclipse GSX?

The 2G (D31A–D39A) Eclipse GSX makes 210 hp and 214 lb-ft. Motor Trend (1996) measured 0–60 mph in 6.4 seconds.

What engine does the Mitsubishi Eclipse (2nd gen) have?

The 2G (D31A–D39A) Eclipse (1995–1999) uses the Mitsubishi 4G63T, a 2.0-liter inline-4, turbo.

Which Fast and Furious movie is the Eclipse in?

The Fast and the Furious (2001): Brian O'Conner's green 1995 Eclipse RS, his first race car in the series. It falls apart when he overuses nitrous racing Dom, who wins it, and Johnny Tran's crew later shoots it up and destroys it. The film car had the non-turbo Chrysler 420A engine, not the GS-T or GSX turbo.

Was the Mitsubishi Eclipse (2nd gen) sold in the US?

Yes. Sold new in the US for model years 1995-1999. It was built at Diamond-Star Motors in Normal, Illinois.

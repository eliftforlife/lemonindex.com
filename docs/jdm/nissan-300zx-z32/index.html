Nissan 300ZX Twin Turbo (Z32, 1990–1996): specs, VG30DETT engine and what owners say

300ZX Twin Turbo Z32 · 1990–1996

300horsepower
5.50–60 mph, sec
155mph top speed

3.0-liter twin-turbo VG30DETT V6, 300 hp (US manual), 5-speed manual or 4-speed automatic, rear-drive. Non-turbo: 222 hp.

Why it's famous

  • Import Car of the YearName

    Motor Trend named the 300ZX its 1990 Import Car of the Year.

  • Seven years on the 10Best listName

    Car and Driver put the Z32 on its Ten Best list for seven years in a row, every model year it was sold in the US.

  • Daytona and Le Mans winnerRacing

    A Cunningham-prepared 300ZX won the 1994 24 Hours of Daytona and the 12 Hours of Sebring. At Le Mans that year, Steve Millen, Johnny O’Connell and John Morton won the IMSA GTS class and finished fifth overall. Millen and Nissan took the 1994 IMSA GTS drivers’ and manufacturers’ titles.

  • Designed on a supercomputerNameNot fully confirmed

    Nissan used a Cray-2 supercomputer to design the Z32, which Wikipedia calls one of the first production cars designed with CAD software.

  • The Super Bowl ad that aired onceFilm

    Ridley Scott directed a 300ZX Twin Turbo ad for the 1990 Super Bowl. It aired only once before Nissan pulled it, reportedly over fears it promoted street racing.

Sold new in the US. Sold new in the US for model years 1990 to 1996, as the non-turbo 300ZX and the 300ZX Twin Turbo. The last 300 US cars included a Commemorative Edition. In Japan it was sold as the Fairlady Z until 2000.

Known issues

  • Turbo failure, and the engine comes out to fix itOutlet report

    SlashGear, citing Car and Driver, says the turbo seals can start leaking between 60,000 and 80,000 miles. Writing on Jalopnik about his own car, Peter Monshizadeh found a seized turbo and says the engine bay is so cramped that the easiest way to replace the turbos is to pull the engine.

  • Fuel injectors and misfiresOutlet report

    Jalopnik’s owner found two dead fuel injectors during his rebuild, and another failed about 3,000 miles later. He says the old-style injectors don’t like ethanol fuel. SlashGear, citing Autocar, says injectors can stick and cause misfires, and Hagerty UK says to listen for a ticking from the coils or injectors.

    8 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (1990–1996)
  • Timing beltOutlet report

    Hagerty UK says a new timing belt is needed every 60,000 miles, and to judge it on age as well as mileage. Jalopnik gives 60,000 miles or four years, whichever comes first. Hagerty UK also says to check for oil leaks at the cam end seals.

    Fix: Replace the timing belt every 60,000 miles or four years.

    1 owner complaint to NHTSA mentions it (1990–1996)
  • Heat, wiring and old hosesOutlet report

    Hagerty UK says this engine runs hot as standard, so the cooling system must be perfect, and that the heat can damage the wiring loom. Jalopnik’s owner found the rubber brake-booster vacuum hoses old and cracked, and says the many plenum vacuum hoses are hard to refit.

    2 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (1990–1996)

Quick answers

How fast is the Nissan 300ZX Twin Turbo?

The Z32 300ZX Twin Turbo makes 300 hp and 283 lb-ft. Car and Driver measured 0–60 mph in 5.5 seconds. Top speed: 155 mph (electronically limited).

What engine does the Nissan 300ZX (Z32) have?

The Z32 300ZX (1990–1996) uses the Nissan VG30DETT, a 3.0-liter V6, twin-turbo.

Why is the 300ZX famous?

Import Car of the Year: Motor Trend named the 300ZX its 1990 Import Car of the Year. Seven years on the 10Best list: Car and Driver put the Z32 on its Ten Best list for seven years in a row, every model year it was sold in the US. Daytona and Le Mans winner: A Cunningham-prepared 300ZX won the 1994 24 Hours of Daytona and the 12 Hours of Sebring. At Le Mans that year, Steve Millen, Johnny O’Connell and John Morton won the IMSA GTS class and finished fifth overall. Millen and Nissan took the 1994 IMSA GTS drivers’ and manufacturers’ titles.

Was the Nissan 300ZX (Z32) sold in the US?

Yes. Sold new in the US for model years 1990 to 1996, as the non-turbo 300ZX and the 300ZX Twin Turbo. The last 300 US cars included a Commemorative Edition. In Japan it was sold as the Fairlady Z until 2000.

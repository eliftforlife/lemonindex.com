Recalled, or still open? Not the same thing.

A recall only matters if it's still open on that exact car. NHTSA's public recall data works by make, model and year, so these lists cover every car of that model year, not one VIN. Whether a recall was already fixed on a particular car is in the automaker's records: type the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls or ask any dealer of that brand. The repair is free.

Questions

How do I check if my car has a recall?

Use its VIN, the 17-character number on the dashboard by the windshield, on the driver's door frame and on the registration. Type it at nhtsa.gov/recalls or ask any dealer of that brand: that shows whether a recall is still open on that car. Our VIN check decodes the VIN and lists every recall for that model year, free.

Is my car recalled if its model is on a recall list?

Not always. A recall covers the cars the automaker names, which can be one engine, one plant or a range of build dates, and your car may already have been fixed. The lists here cover every car of a make, model and year. Only the VIN tells you if a recall is open on your car.

Can I check car recalls by VIN for free?

Yes. NHTSA's lookup at nhtsa.gov/recalls is free and covers recalls from the last 15 years, and so is ours. We don't store the VIN. NHTSA's public data API works by make, model and year, not by VIN, which is why the final check of one car is at nhtsa.gov or a dealer.

Do I pay for a recall repair?

No. The automaker pays: the dealer fixes it free, used car or not. U.S. law (49 U.S.C. 30120) requires a free fix on vehicles under 15 years old when the recall is filed.

What does "park it" or "park outside" mean on a recall?

NHTSA flags the most urgent recalls: "park it" means don't drive the car until it's fixed, and "park outside" means it could catch fire while parked, so keep it away from buildings.

Does this cover cars in Canada?

The recall lists here are U.S. (NHTSA) campaigns. Canadian cars can have different recalls: search Transport Canada's recall database or check the VIN on the maker's Canadian recall site.

Where does this data come from?

NHTSA's recall campaigns (api.nhtsa.gov) for the 339 models we track, model years 1993–2026, and NHTSA's recall file for vehicle counts and park-it warnings (2010 and later). The make, model and year lookup at the top asks NHTSA live, so it's always current.

Source: NHTSA recall campaigns (api.nhtsa.gov), matched to the model names owners use in NHTSA complaints. Vehicle counts and the "park it" warnings come from NHTSA's recall file, which starts in 2010, so older recalls have no count. A campaign's count covers every make and model in it.

How are the picks chosen?

Mostly by reliability: the Lemon Index of those years (owner complaints to NHTSA in the first 3 years, per car sold; 100 is the average car, lower is better), then by how YouTube buyer guides rate them. Each pick is a run of model years from one generation, every one scoring as well as or better than the average car, so you can search for any of them near you.

Where do the prices come from?

They are Lemon Index estimates made with AI for November 2026, not listings or an appraisal: a fair private-seller and dealer price for an average car with a clean title at the mileage most cars of those years have. Check real listings and get an inspection before you buy.

Reliability: the Lemon Index of each model year, NHTSA owner complaints filed in its first 3 years per vehicle sold, against the average car (100). A complaint is an owner's report, not a confirmed defect. YouTube buyer score: how YouTube buyer guides and owner reviews rate those years (Lemon Index analysis of video titles and transcripts). Prices: Lemon Index AI estimates (November 2026) of a fair private-seller and dealer price at the years' typical mileage, average condition, clean title; not listings, sales records or an appraisal. A pick is a run of consecutive model years in one generation, each scored at or better than the average car, with 15,000+ sold each year, whose price (middle of the range) fits the budget. Ranked 60% reliability, 40% YouTube buyer score, plus a little for a longer run. Twins sold under two brands count once. Left out: full-size work vans (mostly fleet-sold). Supercars: YouTube buyer score and price only.

Why our complaint totals differ

Each number counts a different slice of the same government file:

  • 1,607,537: every vehicle complaint in NHTSA's file, 1995 to October 2026, all makes (the study and method pages).
  • 929,012: every complaint about the 260 models we track, model years 2000 to 2025, filed at any time (the statistics page and the “How serious” counts on year pages).
  • 337,224: the same models and years, counting only complaints filed in each car's first 3 years (the complaint count on each year page and the by-year CSV).
  • 176,126: first-3-year complaints for the 130 models in the study, model years 2010 to 2022 (the home page). These set the main Lemon Index.
  • 827,013: every complaint filed at any time for the model years in the transmission CSV (its all_complaints column), so it runs larger than the first-3-year counts.

EV scores use their own window: model years 2021–2024, complaints filed by the end of the year after the model year.

Where the numbers come from

Owner complaints come from NHTSA, the U.S. government's vehicle safety agency, which publishes them for free. The score and the transmission matching are ours. See exactly how the index works.

Not included: car prices, sales figures and complaint text (other sources own those); each model's sales source is linked in the model CSV and on its page. Please don't present the data as an official rating, and don't suggest we endorse you. Using it somewhere big, or want a custom cut? Tell us. We'd love to see it.

Trucks & diesel engines · Ford F-150 reliability by year

F-150 engines: 2.7 vs 3.5 EcoBoost vs 5.0.

Ford doesn't report F-150 sales by engine, so nobody can honestly say which engine breaks more per truck. What we can show: what owners of each one complain about, from 18,026 complaints matched to the engine by VIN. The 3.5 EcoBoost gen 1 draws the biggest share of engine complaints (26%), the 5.0 Coyote V8 the smallest (13%).

Every percentage is a share of that engine's own complaints, counted from the area owners pick and their own words. The 3.5 EcoBoost changed in 2017 (2nd generation, with port injection added), so it has two columns. Raptor (High Output), PowerBoost hybrid and Lightning complaints are left out.

What stands out

  • 2.7 EcoBoost: oil leaks (the oil pan on early trucks), 44 of 2,480 complaints (1.8%) against a 0.3% median for the others.
  • 2.7 EcoBoost: coolant leaks, 39 of 2,480 complaints (1.6%) against a 1.0% median for the others.
  • 3.5 EcoBoost gen 1: intercooler condensation (misfire or stumble under load), 97 of 6,252 complaints (1.6%) against a 0.1% median for the others.
  • 3.5 EcoBoost gen 1: timing chain stretch, 137 of 6,252 complaints (2.2%) against a 0.3% median for the others.
  • 3.5 EcoBoost gen 1: misfires, 166 of 6,252 complaints (2.7%) against a 0.4% median for the others.
  • 3.5 EcoBoost gen 2: cam phaser rattle at cold start, 526 of 3,456 complaints (15%) against a 0.6% median for the others.
  • 3.5 EcoBoost gen 2: the timing chain, 61 of 3,456 complaints (1.8%) against a 0.3% median for the others.
  • 5.0 Coyote V8: oil consumption, 108 of 5,838 complaints (1.8%) against a 0.3% median for the others.

Only problems at least 1.5× the median of the other F-150 engines, with 10+ complaints.

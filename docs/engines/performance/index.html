Performance engines, by year.

48 engines in 115 performance cars, every one sourced. The government files most performance versions under their base model, so each engine leads with its YouTube owner tone, recalls and the automaker's repair notes.

Each engine is matched to its cars from EPA's version list (fueleconomy.gov) and checked against the engine's Wikipedia article or the automaker. YouTube tone = how positive the videos that name the engine or its cars are (0–100), averaged over the scored model years; the YT badge ranks engines by it. Recalls: NHTSA, for cars and years with this engine. Repair notes: the automaker's engine-related notes to dealers (NHTSA manufacturer communications). Owner complaints are charted only where there are 30 or more. None of these is an engine failure rate. Images: AI illustrations, free to use under CC BY 4.0.

Quick answers

Which performance car engine is the most reliable?

We cover 48 performance-car engines, from the Hellcat and the Coyote to the LS family and the EJ257. The government files most performance versions under their base model, so we rank them by YouTube owner tone: the Acura J30AC 3.0 turbo V6 ranks first (60 of 100). Each engine page shows its recalls and the automaker's repair notes by model year.

Why no owner-complaint score for performance engines?

Owners file government complaints under the model name, and a Hellcat is filed as a Challenger, a GT500 as a Mustang. We only count complaints whose model name or the owner's own words tie them to one engine, which is too few to rank fairly, so we lead with YouTube, recalls and repair notes.

Supercar engines, by year.

39 engines in 41 supercars, every one sourced. Supercars draw too few government complaints to score per car, so each engine shows its YouTube owner tone, recalls and the automaker's repair notes.

Each engine is matched to its cars from EPA's version list (fueleconomy.gov) and checked against the engine's Wikipedia article or the automaker. YouTube tone = how positive the videos that name the engine or its cars are (0–100), averaged over the scored model years; the YT badge ranks engines by it. Recalls: NHTSA, for cars and years with this engine. Repair notes: the automaker's engine-related notes to dealers (NHTSA manufacturer communications). Owner complaints are charted only where there are 30 or more. None of these is an engine failure rate. Images: AI illustrations, free to use under CC BY 4.0.

Quick answers

Which supercar engine is the most reliable?

We cover 39 supercar engines, from the Ferrari F154 to the Bugatti W16. Supercars draw too few owner complaints to rank per car, so we rank them by YouTube owner tone: the Porsche 9A2 Evo 3.7 ranks first (64 of 100). Each engine page shows its recalls and the automaker's repair notes by model year.

Why no owner-complaint score for supercars?

The Lemon Index scores cars by government complaints per car sold. Supercars sell in the hundreds or low thousands a year and draw a handful of complaints, too few to rank fairly, so we show the count and lead with YouTube, recalls and repair notes.

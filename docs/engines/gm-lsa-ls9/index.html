Reliability by year

Known issues on record

  • Supercharger rattle at idle (Maker bulletin). GM found the supercharger on 2012–2013 ZL1s (and 2009–2013 CTS-Vs) can rattle at idle. Left alone, it can ruin the bearing grease and, in the worst case, stop the engine. Fix: Dealers replace the supercharger free under a special coverage of 10 years or 120,000 miles. 1 owner complaint to NHTSA mention it (2nd gen CTS-V).
  • Low-rpm buzz on replacement superchargers (Maker bulletin). On cars with a replacement supercharger (solid coupling), a buzz at 800–900 rpm in second or third gear under load is normal, GM told dealers. Fix: No repair; GM said not to replace parts for this noise. 2 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (5th gen Camaro ZL1).

Sources: Motor Authority: GM identifies problem with supercharger on certain Cadillac CTS-V and Camaro ZL1 models (Jun 2014), GM bulletin PIP5192A: Supercharger rattle or buzz, 6.2L LSA, Cadillac CTS-V and Camaro (Sep 2014, NHTSA file). The same list as on the Camaro ZL1 page, the CTS-V page.

Quick answers

What cars have the GM LSA / LS9 6.2 supercharged engine?

In the US: the Chevrolet Corvette ZR1 (C6), Cadillac CTS V / CTS-V, Chevrolet Camaro, model years 2009–2015. See the version table for each one.

Is the GM LSA / LS9 6.2 supercharged reliable?

Mostly, with known issues to check. YouTube owner tone 52/100, #66 of 178 engine pages with a YouTube study (in the middle third); 1 engine-related recall on record, 2 known issues listed below. Not enough owner complaints to judge it per car: the government files most performance versions under their base model. Its cars had 1 recalls (1 about the engine, fuel or powertrain). Have any used car inspected.

How to read this chart

Not enough complaints to chart. Owners filed 19 complaints with the government that we can tie to this engine's cars, 2009–2015, about any part of the car. Performance versions are mostly filed under their base model (a Shelby GT500 is filed as a Mustang), so we only count complaints whose model name or the owner's own words tie them to this engine, and lead with YouTube, recalls and the automaker's repair notes.

Why the car pages show more: the Camaro ZL1 page counts 694 complaints and 9 recall campaigns for 2012–2015, everything NHTSA files as "Camaro", other engines included; the CTS-V page counts 667 complaints and 14 recall campaigns for 2009–2015, everything NHTSA files as "Cts", other engines included. This page counts only the 19 complaints and 1 recall we can tie to the GM LSA / LS9 6.2 supercharged by model name or the owner's own words.

YouTube videos are most positive about the 2013 (54) and least about the 2014 (50); neighboring years share many of the same videos, so small gaps mean little.

How we count

Owner complaints (NHTSA) are filed by model and year, not engine. We count one for this engine only when the model name and year had no other engine (e.g. "488 GTB", "GT-R"), or when the owner's own words name a version that had only this engine that year (Z06, ZR1, Hellcat, GT500 ...). They cover the whole car (wheels, air bags, electrics ...), not only the engine, and none of them is a failure rate. We chart them only for engines with 30 or more, over 3 or more years. YouTube tone = how positive the videos are, 0–100, from titles and transcripts (praise vs trouble words, the title counts 3 times), weighted by views; a year needs at least 3 videos' worth. A video counts for this engine when its title names the engine or a car that only had this engine in the years it names; a video whose title names no model year counts toward every year of that car, so neighboring years share most of their videos.

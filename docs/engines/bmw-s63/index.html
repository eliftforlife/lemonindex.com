Reliability by year

Known issues on record

  • Valve stem seals and oil use (Outlet + parts seller). Heat from the turbos in the V can harden the valve stem seals. Owners report blue smoke and oil use between services, mostly at high miles. Fix: Replace the valve stem seals.
  • Turbo failures (Outlet + parts seller). Turbos can fail when the oil is old or dirty. The parts seller says first-gen S63 turbos fail more often than later ones. Fix: Short oil change intervals help.
  • Rod bearing wear (Outlet + parts seller). Rod bearing wear is a known complaint on early S63 engines, especially when driven hard. The parts seller suggests changing them early on E70/E71 cars. Fix: Preventive rod bearing replacement. 1 owner complaint to NHTSA mention it (E70 / E71 X5 M).
  • Auxiliary water pump recall (2010–2012) (Recall). The circuit board in the turbo coolant pump could short and overheat. In rare cases this could start a fire. It covers X5 M (E70) and X6 M (E71) cars built through November 2011. Fix: Dealer replaces the pump for free (NHTSA 18V-248). 2 owner complaints to NHTSA mention it (E70 / E71 X5 M).

Quick answers

What cars have the BMW S63 engine?

In the US: the M5, X5, X6, model years 2010–2023. See the version table for each one.

Is the S63 reliable?

Mostly yes. YouTube owner tone 53/100, #47 of 178 engine pages with a YouTube study (in the top third); 1 engine-related recall on record, 4 known issues listed below. The most owner complaints (about any part of the car) filed under version names that used this engine were for the 2013 (10). Counts follow sales, so this is not a failure rate. Government complaints are filed by model and year, not engine, and they cover the whole car, so none of these numbers is an engine failure rate. We only credit a number to this engine where the version name or the model year leaves no doubt. Have any used car inspected.

How much horsepower does the S63 make?

BMW rated it at 547 to 617 hp in US versions, depending on the car and year.

How to read this chart

YouTube videos are most positive about the 2023 (57) and least about the 2017 (49); neighboring years share many of the same videos, so small gaps mean little.

Owner score = the whole car's Lemon Index where

How each number is matched to the engine: (a) complaints filed under a version name that used only this engine that year (e.g. "335I"); (b) the model had only this engine that year, so its Lemon Index (complaints per car sold vs the average car) applies to every car with this engine; "all engines combined" = the model had several engines under one name, so its number is not this engine's alone. Owner complaints cover the whole car (brakes, air bags, electrics ...), not only the engine. None of these is a failure rate. YouTube tone = how positive the videos are, 0–100, from titles and transcripts that name the engine or a version that used it. A video whose title names no model year counts toward every year of its generation, so neighboring years share most of their videos.

Toyota 4A-GE engine: known problems and recalls.

Toyota's 1.6-liter inline-4, DOHC 16-valve, naturally aspirated. This page covers the versions we have sourced so far: the Toyota AE86, model years 1984–1987. The engine is used in other cars too; those aren't covered here yet.

Known issues on record

  • Head gasket (Outlet report). DSPORT magazine says the 4A-GE’s head gaskets are prone to failure. Goodwood’s buyer’s guide still calls the twin-cam mechanically tough. 1 owner complaint to NHTSA mention it (AE86 AE86).
  • Timing belt history (Outlet report). Both camshafts are driven by a timing belt. JDM Buy Sell’s buyer’s guide says to ask for dated belt records and to treat the belt as due if there are none. Fix: Replace the timing belt if there are no dated records.
  • Fake or swapped twin-cams (Outlet report). Goodwood warns about single-cam AE85s passed off as twin-cam AE86s, especially when bought unseen from an importer. JDM Buy Sell says a 4A-GE fitted to an AE85 does not make it an AE86, so check the chassis code and papers against the engine.

Specs by generation

Toyota 4A-GE: AE86 AE86, 1984–1987

  • Layout: Inline-4, DOHC 16-valve, cams driven by a timing belt, forged crankshaft
  • Output: 112 hp at 6,600 rpm, 97 lb-ft at 4,800 rpm in the US GT-S; 130 PS (128 hp) at 6,600 rpm in Japan
  • Size: 1,587 cc, 81 x 77 mm bore x stroke, 9.4:1 compression
  • Induction: Fuel injection with T-VIS, butterfly valves in the intake runners that open at about 4,200 rpm; airflow meter on US cars, pressure (MAP) sensor in Japan
  • Parts today: Toyota’s GR Heritage Parts program has remade some AE86 parts, including new cylinder blocks and heads

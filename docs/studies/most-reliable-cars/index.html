Three patterns in the data

  1. New designs are a gamble, but year one is only a little worse. Several of the worst models are recent all-new designs (Telluride, Palisade, Pacifica, the 2014-on Cherokee). Across 31 new models, the first model year drew about 20% more complaints than the next three, so waiting a year helps a little. It isn't a rule.
  2. The Germans beat their reputation, early on. In the first three years of ownership, BMW (52) and Mercedes-Benz (54) drew fewer complaints per car than Honda (87) or Ford (144). This measures early safety complaints, not out-of-warranty repair bills.
  3. Tesla's problem is mostly one system. About 80% of Model 3 and Model Y complaints are about forward collision avoidance, the car braking hard for nothing.

Correction (10-24): an earlier version (and our video) said first model years drew about 70% more complaints. After we fixed a sales-timing error (a new model's first cars often sell the calendar year before its model year), it's about 20%. A few scores moved by 1 to 5 points; the rankings didn't change, except that the Dodge Dart joined the bottom 10.

How we measured it

A complaint counts only if NHTSA received it within three years of the model year, so old and new cars get the same window. Each model year is divided by that year's U.S. sales, then compared with the average car of the same years. Full method and caveats →
